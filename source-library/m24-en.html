Completing the programme: verified results
and release

M24 • 02 Nov 2028 - 27 Nov 2028 • 75 hours

The M24 objective
Bring the learning outputs together into an engineering project: verified requirements, reproducible
data, versioned code, CAD and configurations, a technical report and a defence. The original plan's
outcome is open release v1.0, a technical report and project defence. This month, prepare a
release candidate whose results can all be checked. The owner decides on public publication
separately after reviewing the contents and comments.
The principal devices are the M23 six-legged robot and the robotic hand from the separate
workstream after M17. Each retains its own requirements, limits, calibrations and evidence. A
shared portfolio index links their software components but does not substitute one project for the
other. If either device has not passed mandatory acceptance, state its exact status and completion
blockers; presentation does not turn an unfinished device into a finished one.

What to gather before the first session
Prepare M1-M23 outputs: source code, CAD, drawings, circuits, calibrations, configurations,
simulators, tests, datasets, failure logs and alternative solutions. Verify that files exist, rather than
relying on links in an old report. For the six-legged robot, obtain standing, wave and ripple, slope,
failure-response and energy data. For the hand, obtain evidence for the grasps, force and current
limits, sensors and responses actually included in its OPT project.
Do not begin the month by rewriting the entire architecture or changing documentation tools.
Select a pinned working version, describe its boundaries and fix problems that prevent verification
of claimed functions. Your software-development experience helps with automation, but the
number of unit tests cannot replace physical validity.

What constitutes a professional result
Every important claim traces to a requirement, procedure, configuration, raw measurement and
reproducible calculation. State separately what simulation, rig tests, the physical machine and
external review establish. Include unsuccessful approaches and limitations in the report: when
causes and conclusions are justified, they demonstrate engineering judgement.
M24 ends on 27.11.2028; the original calendar then schedules G3 for 27.11-10.12.2028 and G4 for
10-22.12.2028, 35 hours each. These hours and the hand's separate 120 OPT hours are outside
M24's 75 hours. By 22 December 2028, both systems must have an explicit final status. Study
hours and a portfolio alone do not confer a degree, senior-engineer qualification or research
mastery.

Time allocation
Hours 1-20: 10 hours on requirements, metrics and the verification plan; 10 hours auditing data
and conducting the first reproducible runs. 21-40: 10 hours on safe states, failures and release
contents; 10 hours on repeated checks, versioning and clean reproduction. 41-60: 10 hours on
technical explanation and external-review preparation; 10 hours on the report, diagrams, defence
and review comments. 61-75: 5 hours choosing a professional focus and final criteria; 10 hours
closing comments, checking the portfolio and preparing the G4 handoff.
Total: 35 hours of theory and analysis and 40 of practice. The first three theory blocks each contain
five 2-hour sessions; the last is 2 + 2 + 1. Each 10-study-hour practice block includes setup,
testing, processing, corrections and evidence recording. The 30-45-minute external meeting is
included in the third practical, not added to the budget.

Organising the package
Create one index covering requirements, configurations, tests, raw data, calculations, report,
instructions, comments, release and portfolio. Give requirements stable IDs, such as HEX-STANCE
and HAND-GRASP, and configurations unique revisions. Do not link evidence only to a filename
such as final.csv: include run_id, file hash and system version. Large data need suitable storage
with integrity checks; DVC is useful if it already fits the project.
The first-page dates define the original plan's window. Shared boundary dates, holidays and
separate G blocks do not create extra hours. Place numbered work within available sessions
without double-counting. Use time awaiting a reviewer's response for independent tasks; external
review is incomplete until a response arrives.

Priorities when time is short
First resolve invalid measurements and untested mandatory functions, then an unreproducible
build, then ambiguous instructions and reviewer comments. Website design, complex animation
and an additional demonstration video come last. A narrower verified feature set may be released
with explicit limitations; lowering a mandatory criterion after failure requires declaring the
requirement change.
Keep two lists: v1.0 release blockers and development after the programme. For each new feature,
determine whether it is needed to verify behaviour already claimed. If not, add it to the future
specialisation. This preserves time for both physical projects and final integration.

Requirements, metrics and links to
measurements

What to study
Distinguish verification, checking fulfilment of a stated requirement, from validation, checking that
the system solves the intended task under real conditions of use. A specific trial checks 120
seconds of standing; assessing whether the robot makes repeatable experiments convenient also
requires instructions, preparation and user evaluation. Select a method for each requirement:
analysis, inspection, demonstration or testing, sometimes in combination.
A good requirement identifies the object, action, conditions and criterion. Replace low latency with
the events between which the interval is measured, the clocks used, the load and the limit. Derive
the limit from project dynamics, frequency and loading; a round number alone is not justification. A
specification citation identifies the page, units and applicable conditions.

Task 1. A requirements matrix for both systems
Inputs are the six-legged-robot objectives, hand objectives and actual release candidates. 1) Break
broad claims into verifiable requirements with stable IDs. 2) For each, specify origin, method,
criterion, admissible configuration and required log. 3) Assign PASS/FAIL/NOT TESTED/NOT
APPLICABLE with an explanation. 4) Verify that every feature on the portfolio homepage appears in
the matrix.
Save requirements_traceability.csv. Check that every mandatory requirement has a specific
procedure and result source; NOT TESTED is not a pass. Boundary case: after actuator
replacement, an old calibration and test remain linked to the new assembly. Update links and
repeat affected checks. There is no need to retest an unchanged M1 parser merely to increase the
test count.

Task 2. Audit metrics and units
Calculate latency as t_end − t_start only after establishing clock relationships. Define jitter
explicitly, for example as deviation of cycle period from its target; show its distribution, p50, p95,
p99 and observed maximum with sample count. For angular error, state conventions and units.
Obtain energy by integrating U·I over time; use E/d in J/m only for a valid non-zero distance. For the
hand, define grasp, holding and release success separately.
Save metrics_spec.md and a calculation script applied to one reference dataset with a known
answer. Check that values and units agree across code, table and plot; do not present
small-sample quantiles as precise estimates of rare extremes. Boundary case: a current-recording
interval is missing. Mark energy incomplete or use an explicitly justified estimate with an interval;
do not fill the gap with zero.

Independent stopping, failures and recovery

What to verify
Verify the final physical state, not only a software enumeration changing to STOPPED. Removing
torque may make a walking robot fall; for a hand, it may release or trap the object depending on
mechanics. Each system needs a described path for normal stopping, emergency termination of
force or torque application, and recovery. Support and motion restraint come from the design
verified on earlier rigs.
Independence applies to specific failures. If a hardware path depends on the same board's power
or firmware, show that in the circuit. A second thread in the same process does not make stopping
independent of process failure. Test in the intended protected configuration with an observable
physical indication of the result and a criterion established in advance.

Task 3. Reconfirm the final state
Use the final six-legged-robot and hand revisions. 1) Trace command and power paths on the
circuit. 2) Prepare robot support and a safe test object or fixture for the hand. 3) Repeat normal
stopping and the independent emergency path with the programme operating normally. 4) Repeat
the permitted rig case with the control process stopped. 5) Check button release and restored
communication: the previous command must not resume automatically.
Save safe_state_evidence for both devices with a timeline and observation of the mechanism.
Acceptance: each claimed path actually reaches the described state within justified time and
displacement limits. Boundary case: the log reports deactivation but the actuator still applies force
or torque. The test is FAIL; investigate the hardware path and repeat. Software confirmation does
not replace measurement.

Task 4. Failure, diagnostics and recovery
From the risk register, choose sensor loss, stale telemetry, a stopped node and a configuration
error. Check them first in replay or HIL. For each, record stimulus time, detection, restricted
behaviour and operator action. Physically reproduce only permissible variants using the established
procedure; destructive battery failure and short circuits are unnecessary for verifying software
responses.
Save fault_recovery_matrix and a minimal regression set. Check that after a node recovers, the
system rechecks calibration, mode and data freshness before authorising motion. Boundary case:
different errors need different responses, but one reset button hides both. Separate confirmation
that the cause is resolved from permission for new motion. Retain failed cases and the rationale for
protective behaviour.

A reproducible code, CAD and data package

What reproducible means
Another engineer must receive consistent source code, hardware documentation, configuration
and sufficient data to reproduce the main results. Code, CAD, circuit and calibration have separate
versions linked by a shared release manifest. An STL without its source parametric model and
dimensions does not explain the design; a binary without its configuration version does not define
controller operation.
Distinguish reproducing calculations from saved data from conducting a new physical experiment.
The former can be checked in a clean environment without the robot. The latter requires an
assembly, equipment, calibration and admissible conditions. Instructions must state which results
readers can obtain without hardware and which are confirmed only on your unit.

Task 5. The v1.0 candidate manifest
Inputs are the pinned revisions of both systems. 1) List source code, CAD, circuits, BOM,
calibrations, dependencies, tests, data, documentation and demonstrations. 2) State each item's
version or hash and purpose. 3) Separate raw data from derived plots. 4) Check for dependencies
on local paths and ensure required external files are accessible through the instructions.
Save release_manifest.json and a local candidate archive. Check that every link resolves,
checksums match and release notes describe this exact version. Boundary case: calibration comes
from a different hand despite matching file format. Include the physical unit's serial identifier and
revision and add a compatibility check. A filename does not establish correct configuration.

Task 6. Reproduce in a clean environment
Create a separate working copy or isolated environment, restore pinned dependencies and only the
dataset included in the candidate. Follow the instructions from the start: build, software tests,
selected-log processing, table and plot generation. Documentation scripts must not automatically
connect powered hardware. Hardware instructions require a deliberate mode choice and entry
checks.
Save reproduction_log.md with commands, environment, runtime and omissions found in the
instructions. Check that at least one key plot for each of the six-legged robot and hand reproduces
the same substantive result within the stated tolerance. Boundary case: a notebook displays an old
saved-cell plot while calculation fails. Clear saved outputs, run from scratch and fix the
dependency. A missing raw-data file blocks the claim that relies on it.
For M20-M21 results, include the QP configuration, solver-failure check, unchanged classical
baseline, random-number generator seeds and the OOD/fallback report. Identify learned
components investigated only in simulation. Increased reward does not verify physical operation;
explicitly record lack of hardware authorisation in the report and portfolio.

Technical report and external project
defence

What you must be able to explain
Organise the report around the problem each system solves: objective, requirements, architecture,
methods, experiments, results, limitations and future work. In the architecture, show physical
components, command flow, measurement flow and power circuit. A separate timing diagram
explains the measurement-to-action path; a state diagram explains motion authorisation, stopping,
failure and recovery. Do not combine everything into one unreadable diagram.
In methods, describe procedure, instruments, calibrations, initial conditions and processing. In
results, separate measured facts from hypotheses. Each plot has units, a caption, version and
attempt count; photographs show the fixture. Limitations must name surfaces, loads, durations,
uncertainties and untested modes rather than offer a generic paragraph about future potential.

Task 7. A report that stands on its own
1) Write a half-page account of each system's result. 2) Draw three diagrams: architecture; timing;
states and stopping. 3) Select 4-6 main plots that substantiate requirements. 4) Trace each back to
the raw file. 5) Analyse one unsuccessful design choice and one trade-off, such as speed versus
heating or grip force versus protecting the object.
Save technical_report and diagram source files. Check that a reader can identify the operating
envelope and locate data supporting the main claim within a few minutes. Boundary case: body-tilt
and surface-slope plot axes share the same label. Correct notation and caption, then recheck the
text. Every plotted quantity must have an unambiguous meaning.

Task 8. A 30-45-minute external review
Prepare a compact package in advance: objectives, diagrams, requirements matrix, one successful
trial and one failed trial. Choose someone able to assess at least part of the mechanics, control or
software system. During the meeting, allow 5 minutes for context, 10 for architecture and
limitations, 10 for evidence and 5-15 for questions. Record each objection, affected requirement,
required action and closure method.
Save design_review_minutes with the comments actually received. Check that at least one
technical objection is addressed substantively and the response is supported by data or an explicit
limitation. Polite praise does not replace engineering review. If the person has not replied, status is
pending; you may rehearse independently, but cannot call it an external defence. The owner
deliberately sends invitations and grants access; no automatic mailing is required.

A shared test campaign for two devices

Specification of the integrated work
Create campaign.yaml with conditions and run_id values. For the six-legged robot, test standing,
wave and ripple, levelling, restricted operation without a camera and stopping. For the hand, test
the project's intended grasps, holding, release, detection of an unreliable sensor and failure
response. If the hand is not mounted on the body, do not claim integrated mobile manipulation:
these are two completed devices sharing a verification method.
Freeze version, success definition and trial unit first. For the walking robot, the unit is a complete
bounded scenario with restored initial conditions; for the hand, a specified cycle on a known object
with defined position and load. Reprocessing one file checks calculation reproducibility; it does not
add physical trials. A different hardware revision starts a separate result group.

The 30-50+ series and limits of inference
The overall plan suggests 30-50+ tests or a justified sample size. Plan the sample around the
claim: comparable attempts to estimate success rate and separate boundary conditions for
coverage. M23 data may count if configuration, procedure, conditions and criteria match the final
ones; this reuses evidence rather than adding newly conducted experiments. Define the hand's
series separately, without pooling grasp successes with walking.
An example arrangement is 40 attempts for one frozen six-legged-robot task and a separately
agreed grasp series. If time is insufficient, narrow the claim and justify N, or move the open block
to G4 within the overall schedule. Do not promise a reliability percentage from an arbitrarily small
sample. Every mandatory mode retains its test status even if excluded from the main
homogeneous series.

Execution and calculation
First check instruments on a known signal and run a pilot trial. Then follow a fixed order or
alternate modes to control for discharge and heating. After every attempt, record outcome, initial
conditions and interventions. Classify failures as mechanics, power, communication timing and bus,
perception, control, procedure or data. Categories support repair but do not remove failures from
the final table.
For success rate, report k/N, the confidence-interval method and applicability conditions. If heating
creates serial dependence, do not call individual cycles independent; analyse blocks or restrict
conclusions to descriptive statistics. For continuous metrics, show the distribution across attempts,
units and actual observation duration. Observed maximum current and the hardware's admissible
maximum are different numbers from different sources.

Tests of the evidence package itself

Six required scenarios
T1 - a different configuration. In a test copy, substitute calibration from another unit: hardware
activation must be refused or the report must flag incompatibility. T2 - corrupted raw data.
Change one byte or row in a copy: integrity checks or the parser must detect the problem and must
not present the result as unchanged.
T3 - a missing time interval. Delete part of a log copy: duration, energy or latency calculations
must indicate incompleteness. T4 - an old notebook plot. Generate the report after clearing
saved cell outputs: the plot must be regenerated from data rather than retained from a previous
version. T5 - control-process failure. On an admissible rig, repeat independent stopping and
prevention of automatic resumption.
T6 - reproduction without undocumented local dependencies. In a clean copy, run the
claimed recorded-data analysis without hardware: instructions must work with the specified files
rather than an undocumented author directory. Explicitly identify any access requirement and
provide an accessible example dataset. Save expected outcome, actual result and package version
for each test.

Checking whether claims are justified
Take five main claims from the README and CV. Trace each chain: claim → requirement → test →
configuration → raw data → calculation. If a link is missing, restore evidence, narrow the claim or
remove it. A matching trial supports 120-second standing in this configuration; it does not support
stable operation in every condition. A claim to have developed a controller and compared it with a
baseline must identify your own contribution and comparison conditions.
Distinguish certification, educational verification and operational authorisation. Your tests establish
specific project requirements; mentioning safety does not make them official certification. Name
completed checks directly: independent disconnection, command limiting and response to data
loss. Do not claim more than the methods support.

Closing defects
For every defect, record reproduction, consequences, correction and affected requirements. After
changing the system, repeat the smallest set that demonstrates the correction and absence of
related regressions. A caption edit does not require rerunning the entire two-year plan; a
power-circuit or calibration change requires the corresponding physical check.
The change log identifies results belonging to rc1, rc2 and the final version. Label earlier-version
data retained as comparisons. Release with a known limitation is acceptable only when it does not
conceal an unmet mandatory requirement for a claimed function and is clearly documented in the
instructions.

Assigned reading: engineering release

The references may be in English; the exercises and definitions are presented here in English. Read
only the named sections before the associated work, within the existing 75 hours.

Core reading: three guides
1. NASA - Systems Engineering Handbook. Appendices C, D, E: requirement wording,
verification and validation matrices. Then 5.3-5.5: Verification, Validation, Transition. For tasks 1
and 3-6, link requirements, procedures, results and the handoff package for both devices.
2. S. Chacon, B. Straub - Pro Git, 2nd ed. 2.6 Tagging; 7.12 Bundling. For task 5, freeze the
exact version and verify repository restoration. Reference: 7.4 Signing Your Work; a signature and
verifiable identity differ from a matching hash alone.
3. NIST/SEMATECH - e-Handbook of Statistical Methods. 7.2.4.1 Confidence intervals. For the
test campaign, study a success-proportion interval, small samples and the edge cases 0/N, N/N.
Record assumptions and check the calculation against the log of all attempts.

Terms and definitions

Requirement. A verifiable mandatory system property with unambiguous conditions and a
criterion; a wish without an evaluation method is not a complete requirement.
Acceptance criterion. A predefined PASS/HOLD/FAIL rule for a requirement, including limit,
conditions, procedure and required evidence volume.
Verification. Obtaining evidence that a stated requirement is met; methods may include analysis,
inspection, demonstration or testing of a specific configuration.
Validation. Obtaining evidence that a system suits the declared application under expected
conditions; distinct from compliance with an internal specification.
Traceability. Linking a claim to a requirement, implementation, procedure and result, allowing the
effects of changes and the source of conclusions to be checked.
Evidence package. A consistent set of requirements, configurations, procedures, raw data and
calculations sufficient to verify specific project claims.
Calculation reproduction. Obtaining a result again from specified data, code and parameters in
a documented environment; allowable numerical differences are declared in advance.
Raw data. Primary saved measurements before subsequent processing; they must include units,
time, source and recording conditions.
Data provenance. A record of data sources, acquisition conditions and transformations, linking a
final metric to the original measurement and processing versions.
Release manifest. A machine-readable list of files with purposes, versions or checksums, used to
verify that release contents are complete.
Content hash. A deterministically computed data fingerprint for detecting change against a
trusted value; it does not by itself establish authorship or measurement validity.
Digital signature. A cryptographic link between a message and a private key; attribution to an
author also requires a trusted association between the public key and identity.
Regression check. Rechecking previously verified behaviour after a change to detect unintended
deterioration in the covered scenarios.
Fault injection. Deliberately reproducing a specified fault in a controlled test to check detection,
response and the system's final state.
Confidence level. The proportion of intervals covering the true parameter when the procedure is
repeated under its assumptions; not the probability that the parameter lies inside an already
computed interval.
Selection bias. A systematic difference between attempts included in analysis and the target
population, such as excluding failures; increasing N does not automatically remove it.

Relationship: estimated success proportion p̂ = k/N; also report N, interval method and trial conditions. READY
status does not replace this information.

Motion fundamentals: justifying the robot
model

Required synthesis: explain geometry, forces, motion and measurement

By programme end, you should independently reconstruct the calculation chain: foot or finger
target → admissible joint coordinates → trajectory over time → required torques → actual actuation
→ measured motion. Include one such analysis using leg data and one using finger data in the
report; reuse completed experiments without creating a new test programme.

A reference example without a robotics library
A planar mechanism's revolute joint is at the origin, with x rightwards and y upwards. A point mass
m=0.40 kg is attached to the end of a massless link L=0.30 m. Angle q is measured from the
downward vertical towards +x; neglect friction. For q=π/6, q_dot=0.40 rad/s and q_ddot=0.80
rad/s²: p=(L sin q, -L cos q)=(0.15000; -0.25981) m.
Differentiate the geometry: p_dot=(L cos q, L sin q)·q_dot=(0.10392; 0.06000) m/s. Moment of
inertia about the joint is I=mL²=0.036 kg·m². Dynamics I·q_ddot+m·g·L·sin q=τ_act with g=9.81
give 0.0288+0.5886=0.6174 N·m. The first term accelerates the mass; the second compensates
gravity. A link with distributed mass has different I and gravity terms.
Add a vertical environmental force F_ext=(0;2) N at the end. Its torque about the joint is
τ_ext=p_x·F_y=0.30 N·m. The equation I·q_ddot+m·g·L·sin q=τ_act+τ_ext now requires
τ_act=0.3174 N·m. The environment assists motion; do not choose the sign of JᵀF_ext from
memory without defining force direction.

Four defence questions
Geometry: where do FK/IK and the solution branch come from, and what does the Jacobian lose at
a singularity? Dynamics: which forces are included, and what changes with mass or speed?
Control: which signal does the actuator actually accept, and why might the requested trajectory
be infeasible? Contact: what confirms support and grasp, and when does a static model cease to
describe the experiment?
Prepare movement_defense.md: an axis diagram, notation and units, derivation, numerical
hand-calculation results, an independent software calculation, one real log and model limits.
Recheck velocity using a central difference of FK, the external-force sign using r×F and substitution
of torque into forward dynamics. This examines connections between modules rather than
memorised formulas.

Within 75 hours: 1 hour from 43-44 for the model diagram, 1 hour from 49-50 for rehearsal, and 1 hour from
report work in 51-54 for calculation and evidence (task 7). G3, G4 and OPT retain separate hours. Review
Lynch, Park, Modern Robotics §5.2 and Tedrake: Definitions; derive this example's equations independently
from the sum of torques.
