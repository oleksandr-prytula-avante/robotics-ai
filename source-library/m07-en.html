Measure an actuator on one joint first

M7 • 04 Apr 2027 - 30 Apr 2027 • 75 hours

Main deliverable
Over the month, produce a single-actuator test rig and a model relating torque, speed, current and
temperature. The result answers a specific question: can the selected actuator perform the work of
a leg or finger joint over the required motion and load range? A catalogue torque at stall does not
mean the actuator can hold the robot's body continuously. Define requirements, measure an
available sample, separate known parameters from estimates and validate the model on new data.
Decide whether to buy a complete actuator set only after testing one sample.

What must already be ready
From M6, bring the controller, synchronised telemetry, error counters, timestamps, command
limits, physical stop and verified prevention of automatic restart. From M5, bring link geometry,
mountings and approximate mass. Identify where angle is measured: at the rotor, after the
gearbox or by a separate output-link sensor. This determines whether gearbox backlash is
observable. Entry check: convert degrees to radians, revolutions per second to rad/s and grams to
kilograms, and explain force versus torque. Calculate the static torque of a known mass about an
axis.
Select equipment around the available actuator and its documentation. You need a securely
mounted sample, compatible driver or the smart servo's built-in control interface,
angle/current/temperature measurement, a current-limited supply and a guarded fixture. If these
are unavailable, first complete requirements and a digital model; clearly separate calculated
characteristics from measurements. A bracket may be made on the existing 3D printer after
checking geometry, material and load. A printed part does not automatically have the strength of
an industrial mounting.

Scope of the month
Study the differences between DC, BLDC and smart servo architectures, but experiment with one
type. You do not need to develop your own FOC driver, rewind a motor or buy 12/18 actuators. A
smart servo's internal loops may be closed to the user; measure its external behaviour and
explicitly identify inaccessible internal signals. Low speed, holding and backlash matter for a hand;
loaded torque, repeatability and heating matter for a leg. The method is shared, while
requirements differ.
The final report must show the operating region supported by measurements and the modes for
which data remains insufficient. This plan specifies no universal current, temperature, voltage or
mass limits: derive them from the motor, driver, gearbox, supply and mounting documentation.

How to allocate 75 hours

Balance study and experiments
Hours 1-20: actuator comparison, requirements and preliminary model; 10 hours theory and 10
hours practice. Hours 21-40: measurement fixture, encoder, current, temperature and rig checks;
10+10. Hours 41-60: control loops, limits and repeatable response; 10+10. Hours 61-75:
identification, validation on new data and model boundaries; 5+10. Total: 35 hours of reading,
calculation and analysis, and 40 hours of practice. Count evaluation of raw measurements as
practice, and equation derivation and experiment selection as analysis.
Divide each ten-hour theory block into five 2-hour sessions. In each session, study one mechanism,
calculate its effect for your joint, then prepare an experiment. Schedule ten-hour practical blocks
on Sundays, divided into stages with breaks; 10 hours means active work. The final 5 theory hours
are split 2+2+1. Dates on the first page are the periods in the original overall plan. Shared
boundary dates, rest, holidays and integration reviews are unchanged; place steps in available
study hours without double counting.

A separate record for each experiment
State motor model and revision, gearbox, firmware, controller gains, supply voltage, ambient
temperature, mounting, current sensor and temperature measurement location. Record command
and measured angle, speed, current, voltage and temperature on one time axis, or document
synchronisation. The commands file contains what was actually sent after limiting, rather than only
the original desired profile. Save stop causes and missing-data intervals so fault periods remain
visible in plots.

Repository organisation
Create requirements/ for joint calculations, fixture/ for CAD and assembly, acquisition/ for collection
code, experiments/ for configurations, data/raw/ for raw runs, models/ for parameters and reports/
for plots and limitations. Split data into training (train) and validation sets by whole runs before
fitting. Randomly splitting one run into nearly identical neighbouring training and validation points
is not independent validation. Begin with a simple model and retain it as the baseline before adding
nonlinearities.
If time is short, reduce the number of brands compared, omit an additional actuator type and
simplify the interface. Requirements, measurement path, motion limits, thermal observation and
validation on an independent run remain compulsory. Keep missing hardware measurements as
open items rather than replacing them with arbitrary numbers.

Joint requirements and architecture selection

What to study
Compare brushed DC, BLDC with a separate driver, and smart servos with integrated control.
Consider motor, gearbox, sensor and interface separately: a convenient bus does not guarantee
suitable torque, and a powerful motor does not solve feedback problems. Study the torque-speed
curve, efficiency, rotor inertia, permissible gearbox load, encoder resolution, backlash and
transmission compliance. Distinguish continuous and short-duration operation, and electrical,
thermal and mechanical limits.

Task 1. Load profiles for a leg and finger
Start with the M5 mass and geometry; express unknowns as ranges. For a link with a horizontal
lever arm, use τ=mgr, with m in kg, g in m/s², r the perpendicular lever arm in metres and τ in
N·m. 1. Draw a free body with force direction. 2. Calculate three link positions and two payload
cases. 3. For the leg, add a support scenario with load distribution explicitly assumed; do not divide
robot mass by leg count without describing the support phase. 4. For the finger, use desired
fingertip force and lever arm. Save the requirements table and sensitivity to mass and length.
Check that doubling the lever arm at constant force doubles static torque. Edge case: zero force
lever arm.

Task 2. Compare actuators under relevant operating conditions
Select three architectures or available candidates with primary documentation. Build a comparison
table: torque in the required mode, loaded speed, mass, reduction, sensor, backlash, current
control, diagnostics, supply, interface and unknown characteristics. 1. Reject any option that fails a
hard requirement. 2. Separately assess uncertainty from missing data. 3. Choose one sample for
the rig and describe an experiment that could disprove that choice. Prepare a decision justifying
each criterion; an arbitrary weighted score is no substitute. Check that every important conclusion
has a source, calculation or future test, and that stall torque has not been used as continuous
torque.

Reduction and inertia
Fix the definition N=motor speed/output-shaft speed. In an ideal transmission, output speed is
divided by N, torque is multiplied by N, and load inertia reflected to the motor is divided by N².
Actual efficiency and backlash impose additional limitations. For the chosen geometry, calculate
how increasing the ratio changes speed and reflected inertia. These calculations are hypotheses for
measurement; gear tooth counts alone cannot establish actual holding accuracy.

Measurement rig and calibration

Task 3. Fixture for one actuator
The aim is repeatable motion without movement of the motor housing. Inputs: mounting drawing,
load direction and the sample's permissible operating characteristics. 1. Draw the load path from
shaft through mounting to base. 2. Position travel stops, motion-area guarding, access to the stop
control and cable strain relief. 3. Check alignment and free travel without powered motion. 4.
Perform the first powered run at the minimum justified command, increasing it in predefined steps.
Deliver CAD, photographs, a fastener list and the verification record. Edge case: the cable must not
become taut and alter measured torque at the travel limits.
Create one table of permissible values for the supply, driver, wires, connectors and fuse. The
weakest component determines the limit, considering operating mode, cooling and documentation.
A fuse and a software current limit serve different purposes. Separately establish behaviour during
braking and energy return to the supply: a supply capable of delivering energy may be unable to
absorb it during braking. Do not command sharp acceleration or braking until this behaviour is
understood.

Task 4. Encoder angle and speed
Check counts per revolution, quadrature decoding mode, gear ratio and sign. 1. Apply a known
manual displacement with the actuator disabled, if its construction permits back-driving. 2.
Compare counts with output-link angle. 3. Estimate velocity from angle differences over a known
interval and, alternatively, from time between edges if pulses are available. 4. Compare noise and
delay at low and higher speeds. Deliver the conversion factor and plots of both estimates. Check
that a full revolution or available reference angle produces the expected count. Edge cases:
overflow, reversal, zero speed and a missed edge.

Task 5. Current and temperature
Establish what the sensor measures: supply current, winding current or current in the control
coordinate frame. These quantities are not automatically interchangeable as I in τ=K_t I. Measure
zero-current offset, compare known points with a reference instrument and retain uncertainty.
Record temperature with the sensor location and settling time: housing and windings heat
differently. Deliver calibration coefficients and a description of limitations. Fault: a missing or
saturated instrument must not permit continuation of an experiment that depends on its readings.

Position control and bounded commands

Control-loop architecture
Determine who closes the current, velocity and position loops: your code, the driver or smart-servo
firmware. Use SimpleFOC and ODrive resources to draw the cascade and label the input and output
units of each block. An external controller must not duplicate an unknown internal loop without
understanding its mode. PID relates error to control action; feedforward predicts the required
action. Command limiting introduces nonlinearity: the integrator can continue accumulating error
when the actuator can no longer meet the request.

Task 6. Repeatable position response
Initial conditions: a checked rig, known limits and a small motion profile away from stops. 1. Begin
with an available simple mode and a reproducible initial state. 2. Apply a smoothly changing
command with specified limits; save position, speed and actual control action. 3. Change one gain
or profile parameter while keeping load constant. 4. Repeat the best configuration several times.
Deliver reference/measurement plots, steady-state error, overshoot and settling time. Define the
settling band beforehand in angle units; measure the time until the response stays within it. Edge
case: reversal and return to zero.

Task 7. Saturation and anti-windup
Begin in simulation of the simple joint. 1. Limit available control action. 2. Apply a command that
cannot be achieved quickly. 3. Compare an ordinary integrator with the chosen anti-windup
method: integrator limiting or back-calculation. 4. Restore an achievable reference and measure
recovery time. Save two plots of control action and integrator state under identical conditions.
Check that you can explain why controller gains cannot remove a physical limit. Confirm on
hardware only with a smooth motion profile inside the operating region; deliberately jamming the
shaft to demonstrate saturation is unnecessary.

Task 8. Motion profile and stopping
Set position, speed and acceleration limits from rig requirements and documentation. Generate a
bounded trajectory between two angles and compare it with an abrupt step in simulation. Use only
the permissible profile on the rig. Measure maximum speed, current, error and behaviour near the
software limit. Reject or limit an out-of-range command under an explicit rule and log it; a
mechanical stop is not a routine way to end every motion. Communication loss and restart use the
tested M6 state machine, while the mechanism's subsequent state depends on its load and
construction.

Identification: friction, delay and backlash

A testable model
Start at the output joint: J·q̈+b·q̇+τ_c·sign(q̇)+τ_g(q)=τ_a. Here J is inertia about the axis in kg·m²,
b is viscous resistance in N·m·s/rad, τ_c approximates dry friction, τ_g is gravitational torque and
τ_a is actuator torque. Near zero speed, the sign model is inadequate: rest, breakaway and
backlash require separate observations. If torque is unavailable, use the stock actuator command
as input and identify the closed-loop command-to-angle system, explicitly stating that the model
includes the actuator and its internal controller.

Task 9. Backlash and dead zone
The aim is to understand why a finger ignores a small command and why a leg reverses with a
delay. 1. Reproduce slow motion in both directions over one range. 2. Record input command and
angles before and after the transmission if both sensors are available. 3. Plot output against input
in both directions and identify the region where input changes produce no measurable output. 4.
Repeat with another permissible load. Save dead-zone and backlash estimates with sensor
resolution. If only the motor encoder is available, output-link mechanical backlash cannot be
claimed as directly measured; a separate observation method is required.

Task 10. Steps and frequency excitation
Prepare small bounded steps and a smooth chirp within the region authorised beforehand; chirp
means excitation frequency changes over time. First calculate maximum profile speed and
acceleration to ensure they remain within limits. 1. Record raw responses. 2. Identify delay and
characteristic response time. 3. Fit a minimal model to the first group of runs. 4. Validate it with
another profile and initial position. Save parameters, applicability limits, raw data and residuals. Do
not increase frequency into dangerous oscillation merely to extend the measured bandwidth.

Why not fit everything at once?
Friction, delay, an inaccurate torque constant and a velocity filter can explain similar errors. Check
units and measurement delay first, then a simple linear model; add one mechanism only when its
effect has independent evidence. Save parameters with units, estimation method and a confidence
range or observed spread. Lower error on the training record is not success if a new record is
predicted worse. Show one case the model reproduces and one where it predictably loses
accuracy.

Torque, speed, heating and the operating
region

Task 11. A verifiable torque characteristic
If an independent force sensor and known lever arm are available, estimate torque as τ=F·r_perp.
Begin with a secured rig, small justified loads and known geometric accuracy. 1. Check sensor zero.
2. Measure several safe operating conditions with different loads and speeds. 3. Repeat in both
directions. 4. Compare torque with measured current, accounting for current type and
transmission. Deliver characteristic points with variability and operating limits. If torque is not
independently measured, give a calculated estimate and a method for later verification; do not call
a smart servo's internal “load” indicator an accurate torque sensor without manufacturer
confirmation.

Task 12. Thermal experiment with controlled duration
Choose a repeated profile resembling leg work or finger holding, within documented limits. 1. Begin
from a known thermal state. 2. Record ambient and available-sensor temperatures, current,
operating mode and fraction of time under load. 3. Stop at the preset duration or an earlier
documentation-based stop condition. 4. Record cooling after the experiment. Save temperature
rise above ambient and load duty cycle. A short record showing continued heating does not
establish an acceptable steady-state thermal regime.
The simplest thermal model relates temperature rate of change to dissipated power and heat
removal. In the first version, estimating characteristic heating and cooling times and explaining
applicability limits is sufficient. The I²R loss approximation requires the appropriate current and
resistance; supply current and unknown internal commutation may not provide this information. Do
not infer unmeasured winding temperature from an external sensor without a justified
thermal-coupling model.

A combined operating-range map
Plot measured points in the speed-torque plane, marking current, temperature, duration and
limiting factor. Display catalogue limits separately from your measurements. For a leg, state the
supported profile and tested duration; for a finger, separately assess holding and repeated small
motions. Check repeatability after cooling. An actuator with a different gearbox, voltage or cooling
arrangement is a different configuration requiring verification. The final map informs robot design;
it does not guarantee every future operating mode.

Fault tests and numerical criteria

Measurement path
F1: encoder sign reversed. Detect the error in a software test and an initial small motion before
continuing closed-loop control; record expected and actual direction. F2: incorrect counts per
revolution. Comparison with an independent angle must reveal the scale error. F3: current or
temperature data disappears. Mark the run invalid and stop it if the relevant parameter is a safety
condition for the experiment. Do not reuse the last available value as fresh protective information.
F4: timestamps jump or frames are lost. Do not treat angle differentiation across a corrupted
interval as reliable velocity.

Control and mechanics
F5: target angle beyond the software limit. Check the chosen command rejection or limiting rule;
log requested and applied commands. F6: control action saturates in simulation. After an
achievable reference returns, anti-windup restores normal operation according to a defined metric;
retain the saturation interval in the report. F7: communication loss during permissible motion. Test
the M6 stopping procedure and prevention of automatic restart. F8: small reversal. Compare the
expected dead zone with instrument resolution; the program must not infer absence of mechanical
motion from a single unchanged count.

Validate the model on another record
F9: a model fitted to one profile receives another profile, load or amplitude within its declared
region. Compare angle RMSE, maximum absolute error and transition-time error.
RMSE=sqrt(mean((q_model-q_measured)²)); its unit matches the angle unit. F10: load or
temperature goes beyond the identification region in simulation or existing data. The report must
explicitly label extrapolation rather than conceal it in an overall mean error. F11: power restoration.
Motion must not resume solely from a saved reference.

Set thresholds before the experiment
Choose angle tolerance from the joint's future function and measurement capability; do not specify
“0.1 degree” without reference to the encoder and backlash. Define a settling band and required
time within it before measuring settling time. Compare model error with a simple baseline, such as
a first-order system with constant delay; a more complex model must improve independent
validation. For each experiment, retain maximum measured current and temperature with
duration, sample rate and measurement location. Preserve a failed run with its stop cause and do
not assess it as a fully completed run.

First 40 hours: requirements and rig

Hours 1-10: determine what must be measured
1-2: break leg and finger requirements into position, speed, torque and operating duration. 3-4:
study DC, BLDC and smart servo architectures; draw a motor-gearbox-sensor-controller diagram.
5-6: calculate task 1 static torques and their geometric dependence. 7-8: study the torque-speed
characteristic, reflected inertia and efficiency; plot simple expected trends. 9-10: read
documentation for available candidates and list unknown parameters. Finish by selecting the
question the rig experiment must answer.

Hours 11-20: document requirements and prepare the experiment
11-12: complete task 1 load scenarios and unit table. 13-14: build the task 2 comparison table with
sources and characteristic conditions. 15-16: create a simple joint simulation with bounded control
action; save parameters and hypotheses. 17-18: design the task 3 fixture geometry and check load
transfer, stop access and cables. 19-20: prepare test commands, acquisition configuration and the
first experiment procedure. If the sample is not yet known, remain at interface level and explicitly
mark parameters TBD; do not copy another motor's limits.

Hours 21-30: measurement and protection
21-22: study the supply, driver, wiring, fuse and braking behaviour from the exact components'
documentation. 23-24: study the encoder and velocity-estimation methods; calculate the smallest
observable angle change. 25-26: establish current measurement location and type, sensor offset
and range. 27-28: study thermal modes, temperature-sensor location and housing/winding
temperature differences. 29-30: prepare calibration procedures, initial states and stop conditions.
Finish this block with a usable measurement procedure, rather than notes alone.

Hours 31-40: first physical dataset
31-32: assemble and check the fixture without powered motion, then perform the first
smooth-motion run. 33-34: verify task 4 angle scale and sign. 35-36: compare velocity estimates
and retain stationary noise measurements. 37-38: calibrate current and temperature channels for
task 5. 39-40: repeat a short profile, check synchronisation between fields, stopping and recreation
of the plot from raw data. Saving results is included in every step. Without a real sample, complete
equivalent software tests and leave physical acceptance open until the next available session.

Hours 41-75: control and modelling

Hours 41-50: theory through your joint
41-42: draw the actual inner and outer loops; label inputs and units. 43-44: study P, I, D and
velocity measurement; explain the effect of each gain. 45-46: study action limits, integral
accumulation and anti-windup; prepare a simulation comparison. 47-48: calculate a profile with
speed and acceleration limits; distinguish a software boundary from a physical stop. 49-50: define
response metrics, repeatability, settling band and communication-fault behaviour. Do not attempt
complex tuning until feedback sign and angle scale are verified.

Hours 51-60: control practical
51-52: run the smooth task 6 profile; save the baseline response and several repeats. 53-54:
change one control parameter and compare under identical conditions. 55-56: complete task 7
simulation of saturation and recovery. 57-58: verify the task 8 bounded profile on the rig, including
a request outside the software range. 59-60: perform task 9 slow reversals; assess dead zone and
whether backlash can be measured. Freeze the working configuration at the end; identification
runs must not mix different controller gains without labelling them.

Hours 61-65: prepare identification
61-62: derive the simple joint model and choose a measurable input: torque, current with a
justified conversion constant or an external smart-servo command. 63-64: divide future runs into
fitting and validation sets; choose task 10 excitation and task 11 torque-measurement conditions;
calculate permissible profile limits. 65: finalise the task 12 thermal procedure, report format and
sample-suitability criterion. Split these 5 hours 2+2+1; all following 10 hours are practice, keeping
the total at 75.

Hours 66-75: experiments and independent validation
66-67: record bounded steps and an available frequency profile; estimate delay and initial
parameters. 68-69: obtain available torque measurements or an explicitly labelled calculated
estimate; verify directions and repeatability. 70-71: run the thermal profile for the predefined
duration and also record cooling. 72-73: fit the model and validate on another run; compare
residuals with the baseline and explain one systematic error. 74-75: assemble the operating region,
model limitations and decision on the suitability of one sample. If time is insufficient to reach
steady temperature, state that in the report; a short experiment's results cannot be transferred to
continuous operation.

Presenting the result and moving to
mechanics

What the final package must contain
Requirements for the chosen joint with load scenarios; architecture comparison and sample
justification; CAD and photographs of the guarded rig; supply, stop and limit descriptions with
sources; angle, current and temperature calibration; raw synchronised recordings; controller
parameters; a torque-speed-current-heating model within available measurements; independent
validation and a fault list. For every model parameter, state units, estimation method, operating
range and confidence level. Unmeasured torque or winding temperature remains unknown even if
software can plot it.
Completion criteria from the original plan: selection rests on measurements, electrical and thermal
parameters stay within justified limits, and a set of 12/18 actuators is not selected from one
catalogue number. A negative decision is valid: the sample may be unsuitable because of heating,
backlash, speed, mass or diagnostics. This is a useful completed result if evidence supports the
reasons and requirements for the next sample are stated. Publishing the report here means
preparing a reproducible package; no external sharing or posting is required.

Self-check questions
1. Why is supply current not always the current that determines torque? 2. What changes when the
reduction ratio doubles? 3. How does backlash differ from elastic deformation? 4. Where is the
encoder, and which error can it not observe? 5. Why does a good unloaded response not resolve
leg-support requirements? 6. What does the integrator do during saturation? 7. Why can the same
PID produce a different response after heating? 8. What exactly constitutes independent model
validation? 9. How can measurement delay be distinguished from actuator delay? 10. Why does
housing temperature not guarantee a known winding temperature? Each answer needs your own
plot, calculation or explicit data limitation.

Handover to M8 and M9
Pass axis geometry, permissible angle ranges, sign, zero, resolution and measured dead zone to
M8. Kinematics must generate achievable references within these limits, but does not itself
establish sufficient torque. Pass the identified joint, masses, inertia estimates, friction, delay, input
interface and independent recordings to M9. Keep motor and link parameters separate to avoid
counting the gearbox or inertia twice. When mechanics, load or cooling changes, flag the need for
new validation because the previous operating region may change.

Assigned reading: a focused reading route

Books and notes may be in English; keep the plan, definitions and your explanations in English for
this edition. Read only the specified sections within the existing 75 hours.

Core reading and one reference route
1. Core: K. Lynch, F. Park, Modern Robotics, §8.9. Actuation, Gearing, and Friction: motor, gearbox
and reflected inertia. Read for tasks 1-2 and 9 and explain why motor and output-joint parameters
differ.
2. Core: K. J. Åström, R. Murray, Feedback Systems, 2nd edition. Chapter 11 PID Control: Basic
Control Functions and Integrator Windup. Read for tasks 6-8; draw your loops and compare
saturation with anti-windup. Other editions have different numbering.
3. Core, selectively: R. Tedrake, Underactuated Robotics: System Identification. Sections on least
squares and dynamics identification. For tasks 9-10, list measurable inputs, unknown parameters
and a separate validation recording; skip complex models.

Documentation for the practical work
ESP-IDF: Pulse Counter - Channel Actions and Glitch Filter for the encoder; SimpleFOC: Current
Sensing - motor versus supply current for task 5.
ODrive: Controller - cascade diagram and Trajectory Control for tasks 6-8. This is an architectural
example; obtain your driver's limits only from its own datasheet.

Complete the reading with an action
After each section, perform one calculation or short experiment. Save the actuator, driver and
instrument datasheet revisions. For tasks 10-12, use the familiar SciPy least_squares: check
parameter physical meaning and residuals on new data. Buying printed books is optional.

Glossary: terms and definitions

Torque. The turning effect of force: τ=r×F; torque about a specified axis is the projection of τ onto
that axis, in N·m.
Gear ratio. The ratio of input to output speed; here N=ω_motor/ω_joint, with rotation direction
specified separately.
Efficiency. The ratio of useful output power to input power under stated conditions; it depends on
speed and load.
Torque constant. Coefficient Kt relating motor electromagnetic torque to current under the
adopted model, in N·m/A.
Back EMF. Voltage generated by motor rotation that opposes the supply; proportional to speed in
a simple model.
Stall current. Current in a stationary motor at a specified voltage; this is a limiting condition, not
an allowable continuous current.
Continuous torque. Torque permissible continuously under specified cooling, speed and
temperature conditions; the manufacturer's conditions must be confirmed.
Reflected inertia. Inertia referred through a gearbox to a chosen shaft; rotor inertia at the output
scales as N².
Backlash. The relative angular travel between mating components over which a reversal is not
immediately transmitted to the load.
Static friction. Contact resistance before motion starts; it takes the required value only up to the
maximum holding limit.
Viscous friction. An approximation of motion resistance as torque proportional to angular velocity
and opposing it.
Quadrature encoder. A sensor producing two phase-shifted A/B signals from which angle
increments and rotation direction can be determined.
Counts per revolution. The number of counted signal transitions per revolution of the specified
shaft; it depends on decoding mode and gearbox.
Feedback loop. A path using measured output to change a command in order to reduce a
specified control error.
Saturation. Limiting actual control action at a physical or software boundary, even when the
calculated command is larger.
Anti-windup. A rule correcting integral state during output limiting, reducing excessive error
accumulation and recovery delay.
System identification. Determining model parameters or structure from recorded inputs and
outputs under explicit assumptions.
Residual. The difference between measured output and model prediction for the same inputs,
time and initial state.
Thermal time constant. A characteristic temperature-change time in the adopted thermal
model; it depends on heat capacity and heat transfer.
Check the relationships: P_mech=τω; for an ideal gearbox, ω_out=ω_in/N and torque increases
by a factor of N. Losses and heating require separate treatment.

Motion fundamentals: what a gearbox
provides

Required: connect joint requirements with the motor
Angle specifies the desired pose, but holding and acceleration require torque. For a leg, foot load
and link weights matter; for a finger, the object's force on a phalanx matters. Before calculating,
draw the axis and lever arm: force in N and torque in N·m differ. If the force's line of action passes
through the axis, its torque about that axis is zero.

A minimal transmission model
For one revolute joint, q is output angle and v=q̇ is velocity in rad/s. Choose shaft signs so that
N=ω_m/v>0. Then v=ω_m/N. With power transmitted from motor to load and gearbox efficiency η,
output torque is approximately τ_out=ηNτ_m. Hence τ_out·v=ητ_m·ω_m: a gearbox changes
torque and speed but does not create power.
At an illustrative operating point, let N=20, η=0.75, Kt=0.04 N·m/A, winding current i=1 A and
ω_m=100 rad/s. Neglecting losses within the motor itself, τ_m=Kt·i=0.04 N·m, v=5 rad/s and
τ_out=0.6 N·m. Mechanical power is 4 W at the motor shaft and 3 W after the gearbox. Supply
consumption requires separate measurement of the electrical path.
Separately draw a horizontal link: x right, y up, axis +z and positive torque anticlockwise. A mass
m=0.5 kg at lever arm r=0.1 m produces gravitational torque -mgr=-0.4905 N·m at g=9.81 m/s².
Rest requires compensation of +0.4905 N·m, excluding link weight. The difference
0.6-0.4905=0.1095 N·m does not by itself establish actuator suitability: operating speed, heating
and continuous holding differ.

How inertia affects rapid motion
Acceleration also requires torque I_joint·q̈, with I_joint in kg·m². Rotor inertia reflected to the output
is N²I_motor; load inertia reflected to the motor is divided by N². High reduction therefore helps
produce torque but changes dynamics and sensitivity to contact. Constant η is an approximation
for the specified motoring condition; do not transfer it to rest or reverse energy flow without
verification.

Checks within existing tasks 1 and 11
Build a calculation table and power balance. In the same exercise model, change N to 40: velocity
becomes 2.5 rad/s, torque 1.2 N·m, output power remains 3 W and reflected rotor inertia increases
fourfold. Then insert your confirmed M7 parameters and label unknowns separately. Check
gravitational torque using r×F and the existing task 11 experiment; without an independent
instrument, retain the result as a calculation.
Included in the 75 hours: 1 hour from 7-8 for transmission and inertia; 1 hour from 11-12 for the
load table; 1 hour from 68-69 for checking the existing run. This refines deliverables already
prepared. Exercise values are not specifications for the purchased motor and must not be sent as
commands.
Reference: Lynch/Park, Modern Robotics, §8.9.1 DC Motors and Gearing; §8.9.2 Apparent Inertia.
