Robot state: estimation and admissible
control

M11 • 08 Aug 2027 - 12 Sep 2027 • 75 hours

This month’s deliverable
You will connect three engineering questions: what is happening inside the mechanism, what can
be reconstructed from sensors, and which command is admissible to send. The deliverable
compares PID, LQR, estimation with KF and EKF, and a simple QP on a model with known
parameters, using identical disturbances and testing optimiser failure. For a leg, this prepares for
contact-force allocation; for a finger, it prepares for control using an estimated state with bounded
actuation.
Begin with the axis from M10. State x = [q, v] contains angle q in rad and angular velocity v in
rad/s; input u is torque in N·m; the encoder measures angle. In this model, velocity can be
reconstructed from a sequence of measurements even without a separate velocity sensor. In a
small separate experiment, estimate tilt from a gyroscope and accelerometer. Then solve a planar
force-allocation problem with two supports. These three parts share rules for time, units and
checks, but each needs its own mathematical model.

Prerequisites
Review matrix multiplication, derivatives, eigenvalues and the meaning of variance. Bring the
baseline PID, joint model, command limiter and fixed test scenarios from M10. Distinguish
measurements, simulation ground truth and estimates. Validate estimates against a reference: the
known state in simulation or readings from an independent reference system. The output of an
IMU-processing library is not, by itself, true orientation.

Limits on complexity
This month does not require a complete MPC, a general-purpose 3D-EKF or whole-robot balancing.
Required topics are state space, linearisation, controllability and observability, the linear Kalman
filter, a small tilt EKF, LQR and a convex quadratic programme (QP) with explicit constraints.
Mathematics should explain the experiment rather than block it for weeks. You will check each step
from model to solution and see the limitations of each approximation.
The final project contains code, equations, units, covariances, chosen objective weights, raw logs
and automated checks. All numerical thresholds in example models are experiment parameters.
For a physical actuator, limits come from its specifications, load and the previously validated rig.
“The optimiser found a solution” implies neither physical feasibility nor a correct model.

Organising the 75 hours

Four work blocks
Block A, hours 1-20: state space and observability. Block B, 21-40: KF, EKF and estimation quality.
Block C, 41-60: LQR and a comparison with PID under identical conditions. Block D, 61-75: force QP,
faults and integration. Each of the first three blocks contains 10 hours of theory and analysis and
10 hours of practice; the fourth contains 5 hours of analysis and 10 hours of practice. Total: 35 +
40 = 75 hours. The work steps on pages 9-10 expand these same hours and require no additional
time allocation.
Divide each ten-hour theory block into five two-hour sessions: 20 minutes reviewing the previous
model, 45 minutes reading the relevant section, 40 minutes deriving a small example or examining
a log, and 15 minutes recording the conclusion. Also divide the ten-hour Sunday practical session
into five two-hour periods with breaks between them. Breaks are excluded from net study hours.
Allocate the final five hours of analysis as 2 + 2 + 1.

Start with the question, then choose the tool
The main environment is Python, NumPy, SciPy and python-control; use FilterPy for cross-checking
and CVXPY and OSQP for a small convex problem. Begin each session with a testable question: how
does the input affect the output, which state cannot be measured, and which data reveal its
changes? Before running, write down the expected sign, scale or sequence of events. Compare the
prediction with the result and examine at least one discrepancy. A plot supports a conclusion only
when accompanied by an explanation of the observed relationship.
Maintain one repository with model, config, experiments, tests and reports directories. Every
experiment needs a unique identifier, code version, configuration, parameter source, initial state,
units and raw data. The analysis script reads a saved experiment and builds the report without
manually correcting measurements. The final state-estimation-and-control benchmark project
combines comparisons of estimation and control methods; individual exercises gradually form its
test suite.

Time accounting and reducing scope
Calendar boundaries may coincide with an adjacent module. Keep rest days, holiday intervals and
integration checks as specified in the overall calendar; place numbered steps in available time
without counting hours twice. Do not add hours to this module. Assess equipment and library
readiness at the outset rather than spending the practical session on an unplanned platform
change.
If you fall behind, reduce the number of LQR weight sets, support geometries and validation runs
with different random-number seeds after the minimum checks. Retain the axis model with a
two-dimensional state, an unobservable case, KF with a missing measurement, tilt EKF with an
outlier, an equal-conditions PID/LQR comparison and a two-support QP with infeasible and timeout
statuses. Full 3D orientation and MPC are unnecessary. The result must connect modelling,
estimation and constrained control; all declared results can be obtained in simulation with known
parameters.

State space and the operating point

Study the state and its evolution
Write ẋ = Ax + Bu, y = Cx + Du. For J·q̈ + b·q̇ = u, the matrices are A = [[0,1],[0,−b/J]], B =
[[0],[1/J]], C = [[1,0]], D = [[0]]. Dimensions: A 2×2, B 2×1, C 1×2, D 1×1. Rows have different
physical units; matching matrix shapes do not make q and v interchangeable. In discrete time, use
x[k+1] = Ad·x[k] + Bd·u[k], obtaining Ad and Bd for the declared Ts with a held command.
For a nonlinear model ẋ = f(x,u), choose an equilibrium f(x0,u0)=0 and work with deviations δx, δu.
Evaluate Jacobians A = ∂f/∂x and B = ∂f/∂u at that point. Check the meaning of gravity
compensation in u0 for a loaded axis: do not linearise around an accelerating state and then treat it
as stationary. Small-deviation equations apply only within a limited neighbourhood.

Task 1. A one-step transition
Inputs: the M10 model, two initial states and three constant torques within the model’s admissible
range. 1) Derive the matrices. 2) Obtain Ad and Bd. 3) Compare one step with numerical
integration on a fine grid. 4) Repeat for zero torque and non-zero velocity. Deliverable:
state_transition_tests and an error table. Check: q=v=u=0 remains the zero state; for positive
torque, the first velocity change has the correct sign. Error to test: omitting Ts from an update
creates a scaling error that the test must detect.

Task 2. Observability and controllability
Construct the controllability matrix [B, AB] and observability matrix [C; CA], and calculate rank and
singular values. For comparison, replace the angle sensor with a velocity-only sensor C=[0,1]. For a
free axis without positional stiffness, absolute angle is then unobservable. Specify two different
initial angles with the same velocity and show identical measurements. Deliverable: a short
demonstration using two trajectories, as well as rank(). Check sensitivity of numerical rank to unit
scaling; poor conditioning can hinder estimation even when rank is formally full.
Then linearise a model with gravity near a chosen q0 and compare it with the nonlinear model for
small and large deviations. State the region in which the linear prediction error is acceptable under
your predefined criterion.

KF and EKF: estimation with testable
uncertainty

Study filter prediction and update
Prediction calculates the next estimate and covariance from the model; the update refines them
using a new measurement. Q describes process-model uncertainty, R measurement uncertainty,
and P estimate uncertainty. These are covariances, not “confidence coefficients between zero and
one”. Their dimensions correspond to products of component units. If Ts changes, reconsider
discrete Q using the chosen noise model. Scalar angle variance has units rad²; angle-velocity
covariance has units rad²/s.
The measurement residual, or innovation, ν = z − h(x̂⁻) is the difference between a measurement
and its prediction; S is the predicted covariance of that difference. A smooth estimate can be wrong
even when its claimed uncertainty is small. Check error against simulation ground truth, changes in
P and innovation statistics. For NIS, calculate νᵀS⁻¹ν by solving a linear system without explicitly
inverting the matrix. Comparison with a χ² distribution is meaningful under correct Gaussian
assumptions and the appropriate measurement dimension. This is a diagnostic check with stated
conditions of applicability.

Task 3. Angle and velocity from a noisy encoder
Generate joint motion with a fixed seed, encoder noise and a small model error. Implement
prediction and update for x=[q,v]; compare with a library using identical data. Compare the
velocity estimate with a simple finite difference. Save true and estimated q and v, the diagonals of
P, the innovation and RMSE. Check: noisy data are not written to truth; when a measurement is
missing, perform prediction only and let the corresponding uncertainty grow. Then set R artificially
100 times smaller than the actual variance and explain the consequences using innovations and
estimation error.

Task 4. An EKF for one tilt axis
For one axis, choose state [θ,bg]: tilt in rad and gyroscope bias in rad/s. Prediction:
θ[k+1]=θ[k]+Ts·(ωm−bg), bg[k+1]=bg[k]. For a consistent axis, you may use accelerometer
model h=[g·sinθ,g·cosθ] in m/s²; explicit signs must match the coordinate-frame convention and
rest test. Derive H=[g·cosθ,0;−g·sinθ,0]. Check the Jacobian with finite differences. Introduce an
outlier and translational acceleration; innovation gating must mark rejected measurements and
preserve the prediction. Deliverable: an experiment with gyroscope drift, accelerometer correction
and temporary rejection of updates; yaw cannot be determined from gravity direction alone.

LQR and LQG: the cost of error and control
effort

Study the objective function
For the discrete axis, define the sum xᵀQx + uᵀRu and find feedback u = −Kx. Q is positive
semidefinite and R is positive definite; do not confuse this Q with the filter’s process covariance.
Name them Q_cost and Q_noise in code. To compare rad, rad/s and N·m meaningfully, normalise
components by selected characteristic scales q_ref, v_ref and u_ref. The weights then express the
relative cost of error and actuation, rather than depending accidentally on a conversion to degrees.
LQR does not impose saturation itself. On a real actuator, a limiter changes the closed-loop
dynamics, so test it separately. Tracking a non-zero reference requires deviations from an
equilibrium and the corresponding u0; simply inserting absolute x into −Kx drives it towards zero.
LQG combines a linear Kalman filter with LQR. The separation principle applies under specific linear
conditions; it provides no automatic stability guarantee for delays, saturation, nonlinearities or EKF.

Task 5. LQR on an axis with true state
Use Ad and Bd from task 1. 1) Specify two sets of normalised weights: prioritising accuracy and
prioritising small torque. 2) Calculate K and the poles of Ad−BdK. 3) Check stabilisation at zero and
a small step change of equilibrium. 4) Add the same limit on u. Deliverable: two responses and a
table of RMSE, peak command and the integral u²dt in N²·m²·s. Check: predicted poles are inside
the unit circle for the linear case; under saturation, support the conclusion with a separate
experiment. Failure case: weights designed for degrees are applied to radians without conversion.

Task 6. Comparing PID and LQR under identical conditions
Take the PID with frozen settings from M10. First compare it with LQR under equal access to true
state or measurements; state explicitly which controller receives velocity. Then connect KF and
repeat the noisy cases. Use the same plant, limits, Ts, initial conditions, disturbance and test seeds.
Deliverable: a table of accuracy, control effort, computation time and saturation fraction. Check:
tuning uses a separate scenario set; the final set is not used to fit weights. If LQR wins through
access to true velocity, describe this as an information advantage rather than an advantage of the
algorithm itself.
Separately compare an inaccurate model and estimation delay. Make the conclusion specific: which
controller is better for the chosen axis and conditions, where behaviour differs and which added
complexity is justified. LQR superiority is not a required outcome of the exercise.

QP: allocating forces between two supports

Variables, geometry and dimensions
Model: two point supports in the x-z plane, with coordinates relative to the body centre
r1=(−a,−h), r2=(a,−h), where a,h are in metres. The unknown vector is f=[fx1,fz1,fx2,fz2]ᵀ; all four
components are in newtons. Contact pushes upward: fzi ≥ 0. The prescribed force-and-moment
vector, or wrench, w=[Fx,Fz,My] has component units N, N and N·m. From r×f, obtain
My=−h·fx1+a·fz1−h·fx2−a·fz2. Thus A is 3×4 with third row [−h,a,−h,−a]; the first rows sum fx
and fz.
To avoid directly adding squared N and N·m values, define D=diag(1/Fref,1/Fref,1/Mref). Minimise
||D(Af−w)||² + λ||f/Fref||² with λ>0. Constraints: 0 ≤ fzi ≤ Fmax_i and −μfzi ≤ fxi ≤ μfzi; μ is
dimensionless. This is a planar friction cone with linear boundaries and a convex QP. Label
a,h,Fref,Mref,Fmax and μ as illustrative values or measurement-derived values. Contact forces do
not yet convert automatically into real actuator commands; that will later require Jacobians and
model validation.

Task 7. A feasible allocation
Take symmetric geometry and request Fx=0, Fz=mg, My=0. 1) Write A and the bounds. 2) Solve
the QP. 3) Check fz1=fz2 for symmetric limits. 4) Add a small My and explain the load transfer. 5)
Increase Fx until the friction boundary is reached. Deliverable: a computational notebook, forces at
each support, a normalised residual and active constraints. Check: independently recalculate Af,
every bound and the moment sign. Do not trust solver status alone: check residuals in physical
units with an explicit tolerance.

Task 8. An unreachable request and solver failure
The problem with the soft objective above usually has a feasible solution f=0, so separately add
the equality Af=w and require Fz>Fmax1+Fmax2 to create a deliberately infeasible request. Then
check premature termination at the iteration limit and an artificially late result. Record status,
residual, iteration count and time; compare cold and warm starts on the same request sequence.
Deliverable: p50, p95, p99, maximum and the fraction of unusable solutions. The fallback mode
uses only a previously validated action for the current constraints; a previous solution cannot be
repeated unconditionally after losing contact.

A common experiment and component
compatibility

Three connected experiments
Part A - axis: model → sensor → KF → PID or LQR → limiter → model. Part B - tilt: gyroscope and
accelerometer → EKF → log of estimates, errors and rejected updates. Part C - force allocation:
requested wrench → QP → independent constraint check → admissible output or fallback mode.
Align units, timing and scenario format. QP forces cannot be inserted into the joint model without a
physically justified transformation; a full model of a balancing robot is not required this month.
The axis scenario contains J, b, Ts, noise, initial state, u limits, seed and reference trajectory. The
EKF scenario contains the θ trajectory, true gyroscope bias, noise intensities and intervals of
additional acceleration. The QP scenario contains support coordinates, μ, force limits, requests and
time limits. Label all synthetic parameters as synthetic; measured contains only physically
measured data.

Meaningful metrics
For the axis, calculate angle and velocity RMSE, peak command, the integral of squared torque and
saturation fraction. For state estimation, calculate RMSE against simulation ground truth,
innovation mean and temporal correlation, NIS and the fraction of rejected updates. For QP,
calculate the wrench residual in physical and normalised units, maximum bound violation, solution
time, iteration count, status and the number of transitions to fallback mode. Laptop timing applies
only to that machine and configuration and does not guarantee the same speed on a
microcontroller.
Use one set of seeds for parameter selection and a separate held-out set for final validation. Before
increasing the run count, try small limiting cases: zero state, zero load, symmetric supports and a
missing measurement. Save at least one case you cannot explain and make it an open task with a
minimal reproducing example.

Files and reproduction
The README describes mathematical models and applicability limits; config stores units and
normalising scales; tests checks transitions, Jacobians, matrix dimensions and constraints. reports
contains matching plots for all compared algorithms and a separate fault page. The reproduction
command must recalculate aggregates from saved logs; repeating a simulation with pinned
versions and seed must give statistically and numerically consistent results. Do not require bitwise
equality across different BLAS libraries, operating systems and solver versions without a separate
check.

Six tests for incorrect solutions

Estimation and modelling
F1 - unobservable absolute angle. Two states differ only in q; the sensor sees v on a free axis.
Expected: identical measurements; the estimator must not confidently “recover” an unknown
offset without additional information. Deliverable: two trajectories and an explanation of which
sensor or constraint would change the situation.
F2 - missing encoder samples. Remove several consecutive measurements while retaining the true
motion. Expected: prediction continues, updates are skipped and uncertainty changes according to
the model. An incorrect variant repeats an old measurement as new and artificially reduces P;
include it as a negative test.
F3 - an IMU outlier and linear acceleration. Introduce one large outlier and a separate sustained
acceleration interval. Expected: gating flags the outlier; do not hide the sustained effect by
claiming that an accelerometer always measures gravity alone. Compare the number of rejected
updates and accumulated drift; separately record resumption of updates.

Control and optimisation
F4 - inaccurate model and LQR saturation. Increase J, add delay and constrain u to the same limit
as PID. Expected: measurable changes; the original model’s linear poles are not used as proof of
stability for the saturated system. The report shows where the chosen controller’s advantage
disappears.
F5 - an infeasible QP with hard equality constraints. Request a vertical force above the sum of the
limits with Af=w. Expected: an unusable status is handled before publishing a command; the log
contains a reason rather than an empty vector. Alongside it, show the soft problem with a non-zero
residual to distinguish an optimal approximation from exact fulfilment.
F6 - a late or non-finite solution. Substitute NaN for the output or delay it until the next step.
Expected: checks of finiteness, compatibility with current contacts and the computation deadline
reject the result. Also recheck the last admissible solution against new constraints; if a support has
disappeared, its old forces are no longer permitted.

Analysis and correction
For each test, save inputs, the expected event and the actual event. Check dimensions and
normalisation first, then the model, and only then Q, R or solver settings. Do not reduce R merely
because an estimate looks smoother, or weaken physical constraints to remove an infeasible
status. The corrected version must pass the same original scenario; any criterion change requires
an explanation and a new experiment identifier.

Acceptance: explain and reproduce the
solution

What to demonstrate without prompting
Begin with the axis’s two-dimensional state and give every matrix dimension. Show an
unobservable case, then a successful KF and a poor covariance choice. Explain the EKF Jacobian
and demonstrate its comparison with finite differences. Next, show an identical test for PID and
LQR and a solved QP with a manual check of total force and moment. Finish with a forced solver
failure and the log of transition to fallback mode.
Readiness criteria: state-transition tests pass; every sensor has a measurement model and units;
covariance is not treated as an arbitrary confidence percentage; innovations and missing
measurements have been investigated; tuning is separate from validation; QP constraints are
checked independently; timeout, infeasible and non-finite results are handled. Justify how well
innovation statistics agree with the assumed noise model. A histogram’s appearance alone is
insufficient.

Self-check questions
1) Why can two different states produce the same measurement? 2) How does poor conditioning
differ from formal rank deficiency? 3) When is linearisation needed, and how do you check its
validity region? 4) Why can a filter not keep updating indefinitely with an old number after a packet
is lost? 5) How does Q_noise differ from Q_cost? 6) What are the units of R for an accelerometer?
7) Why does increasing the filter’s confidence not guarantee a smaller error? 8) Where is LQR
action limited? 9) What changes when an angle is converted to degrees without normalisation? 10)
Why can a QP with a soft residual penalty remain feasible for an infeasible request? 11) Why do
force and moment need different cost scales? 12) When can the last successful solution no longer
be used? Relate every answer to a saved negative test.

Handover to the next module
Prepare field-level definitions of StateEstimate, SensorSample, ControlRequest and ControlStatus:
timestamp, coordinate frame or axis, units, value, covariance or quality status, and scenario
identifier. Do not create ROS interfaces before M12, but record which component produces each
field and which state is considered stale. Separately list frequency, admissible age and diagnostic
requirements.
Numerical experiments do not close unfinished physical checks. As specified by the final project,
this month can be completed entirely on models with known parameters; transfer to real
equipment remains a separate, justified step. If reducing scope, retain one linear axis, one tilt EKF
and a two-support QP. Complex three-dimensional models and rewriting all algorithms in a third
language are optional.

Assigned reading: a focused reading route

Books and lecture notes may be in English; keep the plan, definitions and your own explanations in
English. Read only the specified sections within the existing 75 hours.

Core reading and one reference route
1. Core: S. Lall, Stanford EE363: Linear Dynamical Systems, 2026. Slides 9 (discrete systems),
11-12 (controllability and observability), 18 (Infinite horizon linear quadratic regulator), 22 (The

Key terms and definitions

State. A vector of variables sufficient to predict the model’s subsequent motion given known
future inputs.
Operating point. The state and input around which a system is studied; equilibrium additionally
requires an unchanging state.
Linearisation. A local approximation of nonlinear equations using first derivatives with respect to
state and input around a chosen operating point.
Controllability. The ability to move a linear model between arbitrary states in finite time using an
input admissible under the problem formulation.
Observability. The ability to determine a model’s initial state uniquely from its output history with
known inputs.
Covariance matrix. A matrix describing the joint variability of random errors; the diagonal
contains variances, and each element’s units are products of component units.
Process noise. A random input representing uncertainty in modelled state evolution; its
covariance is denoted Q_noise.
Measurement noise. The random component of sensor error in the observation equation; its
covariance R_noise refers to the measured quantities.
Innovation. The difference between a new measurement and the predicted measurement before
updating; it signals disagreement between data and prediction.
Kalman gain. The matrix mapping innovation to a state correction, determined by the
measurement model and estimated covariances.
Kalman filter (KF). Recursive state estimation for a linear model through prediction and update;
optimality requires stated noise assumptions.
Extended Kalman filter (EKF). An estimator for a nonlinear model using local Jacobians to
propagate covariance and update from measurements.
LQR. Feedback that minimises a quadratic cost of state and actuation for a linear model under
specified problem conditions.
LQG. The combination of LQR and Kalman estimation in a linear problem with Gaussian noise and a
quadratic criterion.
Quadratic programming (QP). Optimisation of a quadratic objective subject to linear equalities
and inequalities; convexity requires a positive semidefinite quadratic form.
Feasible solution. A variable vector satisfying every problem constraint within declared
numerical tolerances; optimality is checked separately.
Constraint residual. A quantitative measure of equality or inequality violation, calculated from
returned variables independently of the solver message.
Fallback control. A predefined admissible action when estimation or optimisation fails, consistent
with the system’s current limits.
Check the relationships: x[k+1]=Ad·x[k]+Bd·u[k]; with u=-Kx, the closed-loop matrix is Ad-BdK.
Command limiting changes the linear model.

Motion fundamentals: state and support
forces

Required: what a sensor allows you to reconstruct
Joint state x = [q, v] stores angle and velocity for prediction. One angle measurement does not give
velocity; angle history and a known input allow it to be reconstructed. Before calculating, explain
why the same q with different v produces different leg motion, and draw the ground forces on the
body through two supports.

From measurement history to velocity
A fixed-base joint without friction or gravitational torque: q′ = v, Jv′ = u. q is in rad, v in rad/s, J in
kg·m² and u in N·m with the same positive direction as q. For constant u over interval Ts, the exact
model is q1 = q0 + Ts·v0 + Ts²u/(2J); v1 = v0 + Ts·u/J. Time, input and parameters must be
known.
Let J = 0.02 kg·m², Ts = 0.10 s and u = 0.04 N·m; the sensor reports q0 = 0.20 and q1 = 0.24 rad.
The acceleration contribution to angle is 0.01 rad. Therefore v0 = (0.24 − 0.20 − 0.01)/0.10 = 0.30
rad/s, and v1 = 0.50 rad/s. A simple angle difference would give the mean velocity 0.40 rad/s,
rather than v0 or v1.
When measuring q, the two observability rows are [1, 0] and [1, Ts]; they are independent for Ts ≠
0. When measuring only v, both rows are [0, 1]: an arbitrary constant shift in q is invisible. A
Kalman filter cannot create this missing information. With noise, reconstruction becomes
estimation with uncertainty, making R, Q and timestamps essential.

From desired motion to admissible forces
Model two point supports in a right-handed frame: x forward, z upward, moment My about +y.
From the centre of mass to the supports, r1 = (−a, 0, −h), r2 = (a, 0, −h). Contact forces f act
from the ground on the robot. If horizontal forces are zero, Fz = fz1 + fz2 and My = a(fz1 −
fz2). These are force and moment sums; QP seeks an admissible allocation.
For m = 2 kg, g = 9.81 m/s², a = 0.20 m, Fz = mg = 19.62 N and My = 0.40 N·m, obtain fz1 − fz2 =
2 N, hence fz1 = 10.81 N, fz2 = 8.81 N. At My = 0, both forces are 9.81 N. A non-zero resultant
moment specifies angular acceleration of the body; this is no longer static equilibrium. With a limit
of 12 N per support, a request Fz = 30 N is infeasible because the total cannot exceed 24 N.
Support forces are not yet joint commands. Their generalised external action is Jᵀf; the actuator
accounts for gravity and required dynamics, and opposes external action when holding. Check only
force allocation here.

Exercises and verifiable results
1. In task 2, compare initial q = 0.20 and 0.70 rad with the same v and input. Save two angle and
velocity histories: v measurements will agree, while q differs by 0.50 rad. 2. In task 7, compare the
manual forces with the solver and record sums and moment; then, in task 8, request 30 N with a
hard equality and check infeasible. For the soft problem, check the residual rather than expecting
the same status.

from 15-16 (task 2) and 66-67 (task 7). These calculations belong to existing tasks within the 75
hours; floating-base dynamics comes later.
