ROS 2 as a testable robot software system

M12 • 12 Sep 2027 - 14 Oct 2027 • 75 hours

This month’s deliverable
You will combine individual models, drivers and controllers into a reproducible multi-node system.
The final telemetry-and-control stack v1.0 includes custom interfaces where needed, specified QoS,
separated callbacks, coordinate transforms at the correct time, a software simulator of the
hardware with lifecycle management, diagnostics and replay of a recorded fault. This is the
software foundation for the legs and hand; one leg and one representative finger in separate
namespaces are sufficient for now.
Use this architecture: an IMU or joint data source publishes data; an estimator outputs state; a
controller produces a bounded request; the hardware layer executes authorised commands; a
monitor checks freshness and state; and a recorder saves the experiment. One component owns
each physical resource. Activate control after checking readiness; the presence of a topic, a
message publication channel, in the graph does not establish sensor or actuator readiness.

Environment and prerequisites
The main programme route uses ROS 2 Jazzy on Ubuntu 24.04. Packages support amd64 and
arm64; on an Apple Silicon MacBook, use an ARM64 Linux environment. State the virtualisation
method, network settings and device access. Test DDS participant discovery, USB passthrough to
the guest, graphics and tracing in a small experiment: their operation depends on the virtual
environment. If the graphical interface causes problems, first run the system from the console and
record data, then state graphics and physical-hardware limitations separately.
Previous months must provide stable telemetry fields, units, monotonic measurement timing,
controller and state-estimation models, and negative tests. Do not rewrite the mathematics when
moving to ROS. Extract validated calculations into a library and call it from a small node so the test
without ROS continues to work. Use C++ for execution-time measurement and the hardware layer,
and Python for scenarios and analysis; a third language is optional.

Completion criteria
The system builds and launches from its documentation; the graph and interface ownership are
clear; QoS and execution choices are justified by measurements. The simulator activates and
deactivates correctly, and a recorded failure can be replayed. Prepare material for the architectural
review and recording replay at checkpoint G2. Its integration hours are separate from these 75
hours. A simulator running in ROS does not close the separate HIL criteria involving real hardware.
State every unconfirmed property in the report.

Organising the 75 hours

Four work blocks
Block A, hours 1-20: graph, messages and launch. Block B, 21-40: DDS, QoS and callbacks. Block C,
41-60: TF2, URDF and hardware-component lifecycle. Block D, 61-75: diagnostics, recording and
replay. Each of the first three blocks contains 10 hours of theory and analysis and 10 hours of
practice; the fourth contains 5 hours of analysis and 10 hours of practice. Total: 35 + 40 = 75
hours. The work steps on pages 9-10 expand these same hours and require no additional time
allocation.
Divide each ten-hour theory block into five two-hour sessions: 20 minutes reviewing the previous
model, 45 minutes reading the relevant section, 40 minutes deriving a small example or examining
a log, and 15 minutes recording the conclusion. Also divide the ten-hour Sunday practical session
into five two-hour periods with breaks between them. Breaks are excluded from net study hours.
Allocate the final five hours of analysis as 2 + 2 + 1.

Start with the question, then choose the tool
The main environment is ROS 2 Jazzy on Ubuntu 24.04, colcon, rclcpp for C++ and rclpy for Python;
also use rosbag2, ros2_control, tf2 and one plotting tool. Begin each session with a testable
question: how does the input affect the output, which state cannot be observed, and which data
reveal its changes? Before running, write down the expected sign, scale or sequence of events.
Compare the prediction with the result and examine at least one discrepancy. Accompany every
plot with an explanation of the observed relationship.
Maintain one repository with model, config, experiments, tests and reports directories. Every
experiment needs a unique identifier, code version, configuration, parameter source, initial state,
units and raw data. The analysis script reads a saved experiment and builds the report without
manually correcting measurements. The final ROS 2 telemetry-and-control stack v1.0 project
combines telemetry and control; individual exercises gradually form its test suite.

Time accounting and reducing scope
Calendar boundaries may coincide with an adjacent module. Keep rest days, holiday intervals and
integration checks as specified in the overall calendar; place numbered steps in available time
without counting hours twice. Do not add hours to this module. Assess equipment and library
readiness at the outset rather than spending the practical session on an unplanned platform
change.
If you fall behind, reduce the dashboard to four plots and retain one hardware simulator and one
way of composing nodes in a process. Preserve the interface-ownership table, QoS experiment,
callback-delay measurements, TF at measurement time, lifecycle check and automated fault
replay. A second visualisation tool and porting nodes to Rust are optional. If tracing or a physical
driver is unavailable, name the specific untested criterion; architectural validation on a simulator
does not replace HIL.

Graph, interfaces and launch

Study how nodes interact
A topic suits an IMU or joint-state stream; a service suits a short configuration or state request; an
action suits a long-running motion with feedback and cancellation. A parameter stores a setting but
does not replace a reference stream on every cycle. Study nodes, topics, services, actions and
parameters, then launch, namespaces and composition of nodes within one process. Composition
changes data transfer and failure consequences; the number of logical components need not equal
the number of processes.
First use standard sensor_msgs/Imu and JointState messages wherever they express the intended
meaning accurately. A custom message is needed for fields missing from the interface description:
command identifier, failure reason, state version or associated quality flag. Study conversion of
.msg, .srv and .action to IDL, bounded arrays and strings, and serialisation. State units and
coordinate frames for every quantity, and the time source for timestamp. Version semantic
changes, as well as package names.

Task 1. Interfaces for one leg and finger
Inputs: definitions from M11, one leg with known joint names and one software finger simulator. 1)
Draw a graph containing source, estimator, controller and monitor. 2) Create a field-ownership
table. 3) Create an interface package and two minimal publishing and checking nodes. 4) Launch
/leg and /hand in separate namespaces. Deliverable: an interface document, messages and a
working example. Check: angles in rad, gyroscope angular velocity in rad/s and acceleration in
m/s²; map joints by name rather than arbitrary array position. Error to detect: graph inspection
reveals two publishers simultaneously setting the same reference configuration.

Task 2. Controlled launch and cancellation
Create a launch file for two rig instances, YAML parameters and an action for a small simulated
joint motion. Handle goal, feedback, result and cancel; after cancellation, the simulator must enter
the specified stopped state. Validate parameters before activation. Save one launch command and
a cancellation scenario. Check: package resources can be found from another working directory;
reject an invalid limit or unknown joint name with a clear reason. Endless respawn must not hide
errors, and restarting must not resume motion on its own.
Separately compare nodes in different processes with components in one container. At this stage,
measure correctness of names and data; assess performance gains from intra-process
communication through measurements in the next block.

DDS, QoS and callback execution

Study delivery versus freshness
RMW connects the ROS API to middleware; DDS discovery finds participants, and a domain
separates graphs on the chosen network. Record RMW_IMPLEMENTATION, ROS_DOMAIN_ID and
network conditions. QoS includes reliability, durability, history and depth, deadline, lifespan and
liveliness. Study compatibility between offered and requested profiles: incompatible nodes can
appear in the graph without exchanging messages. Reliable mode does not guarantee that a
command reaches the application fresh by its deadline.
For a sensor stream, freshness may take priority over complete delivery; motion commands need
an explicit sequence number, age and retry policy. A new subscriber must not accidentally replay
an old stored command because of transient_local. A deadline event reports a violation of expected
periodicity, but the application implements stopping. History depth N limits the message queue but
does not itself specify maximum delay. Check the combination of policy and actual behaviour of
the chosen RMW.

Task 3. QoS profiles and message loss
A source publishes N numbered measurement packets at a known frequency. Create three
compatible profile pairs and one deliberately incompatible pair; add a slow subscriber and a late
start. Record transmitted, received, duplicate and missing sequence numbers, message ages and
QoS events. Deliverable: a profile → observed behaviour table. Check: count missing packets by
seq within one source and session; do not treat a restart as a huge negative counter jump. Error
check: a bag recording made with incompatible recorder QoS cannot be considered complete.

Task 4. Callbacks that delay control
Implement a fast telemetry callback and deliberately slow processing or a service. Compare
SingleThreadedExecutor, MultiThreadedExecutor with one mutually exclusive group, and a version
with separate groups. In C++, measure callback start and end, queue waiting time and
publication-to-callback-start delay on a common time scale. Save p50, p95, p99 and maximum
distributions and a group diagram. Check: additional threads need not speed up callbacks in one
mutually exclusive group; a blocking request from a callback must not cause an unnoticed
deadlock.
Repeat the correct configuration with intra-process communication and state the message-transfer
method actually supported. Compare copy counts and latency only for the measured configuration:
do not treat zero-copy transfer as a property of every ROS2 exchange. Isolating computation helps,
but does not itself turn a general-purpose operating system into a hard real-time system.

TF2, URDF/Xacro and the hardware lifecycle

Study coordinate transforms at the correct time
Describe the tree base_link → imu_link and base_link → hip → knee → foot; use separate names for
the finger. TF2 stores timestamped transforms and answers queries for a selected instant. The
latest available transform may differ from the transform at measurement time. Study static and
moving coordinate frames, buffering, timeouts, extrapolation and handling of a missing link. An
incorrect coordinate frame or timestamp can cause a spatial error that resembles a filter error.
In URDF/Xacro, distinguish visual, collision and inertial. Mass is in kg, centre of mass in metres and
inertia in kg·m²; check the tensor’s positivity and physical meaning. A detailed mesh may be used
for visual, with a simplified mesh for collision. The joint axis and limits must match the CAD model
and actuator. ros2_control provides hardware state and command interfaces; read and write
connect them to the device. A hardware component’s lifecycle and an ordinary node’s lifecycle are
related by the architecture but belong to different objects.

Task 5. Foot position at measurement time
Use the M5 geometry and a test joint trajectory. 1) Create a leg Xacro and a separate IMU
coordinate frame. 2) Publish joint states and tree transforms. 3) Transform a foot point into
base_link at the measurement time specified by timestamp. 4) Compare the result with a hand
calculation and kinematics from earlier months. Save the URDF, frame tree and coordinate test.
Check: a known zero configuration gives the expected values in metres; a late measurement
requests a historical transform. If a transform is missing or stale, produce a diagnosable failure
rather than a zero point.

Task 6. Hardware simulator: preventing premature commands
Create a minimal ros2_control hardware component with position state and command interfaces
for selected joints. Configure checks names and limits; activate enables commands after obtaining
an initial state; deactivate puts the simulator in the specified mode. Read returns synthetic
feedback; write applies limits and updates a counter. Save the package, controller manager
configuration and transition test. Check: no motion is executed before activation; a read or write
error triggers the expected state and event. The simulator validates interfaces and logic; check the
real driver, delays and physical stopping separately.

Diagnostics, tracing and fault replay

Study tools for observing the software
A log explains an individual event; a diagnostic status describes component state; a metric shows
behaviour over an interval; tracing reveals execution order and duration. Monitor IMU and
joint-data freshness, hardware-layer state, controller errors, missing-message counters, command
age and recording load. Every warning must contain a reason, timestamp and recovery condition. A
permanent “OK” line does not provide this information.
Rosbag2 records messages and their timing information. Save parameters, code versions,
random-number seeds, hardware events and external clocks separately. Reprocessing a recording
does not guarantee the same thread-scheduling order. For a reproducible experiment, fix the initial
state, use_sim_time, /clock source and input-order rules. Events sharing a timestamp may require a
custom seq or a deterministic handler in the test.

Task 7. Recording, dashboard and causal sequence
Record IMU data, joint states, estimates, commands, diagnostics and the required TF. Save
parameters and a version manifest. In PlotJuggler or Foxglove, create one dashboard: reference
and angle, IMU age, hardware-layer state and controller error. Then delay the source and trace
sensor data stale → monitor detects → command blocked. Deliverable: a bag recording, dashboard
configuration and a brief report. Check: the original measurement sequence number and every
transition are visible; the event does not disappear when switching plots. Compare source and
recording counters to assess bag completeness.

Task 8. Automated fault replay
Create a launch_testing test: start the system with the simulator, wait for readiness, replay a fixed
bag and verify the fault event. Add a timeout and terminate all processes. Separately enable
ros2_tracing for one callback-delay case if the Linux environment supports the chosen tracing
mechanism; state environment limitations. Check the exit code, result contents and system state
after the test. If a node fails to become ready or hangs during shutdown, the test must fail rather
than wait indefinitely.
Define the required repeatability: matching diagnostic events and values within tolerance, or an
identical sequence of processed messages. Do not call a recording deterministic merely because its
file is unchanged. State the controlled sources of nondeterminism and any remaining differences in
delays measured by physical clocks.

Requirements for the ROS 2 v1.0 system

Packages and responsibilities
robot_interfaces contains only messages, services, actions and unit descriptions. robot_description
stores Xacro and meshes. robot_core contains calculations without ROS dependencies; robot_nodes
calls them from estimator, controller and monitor nodes. robot_hardware provides the hardware
simulator; robot_bringup stores launch and YAML; robot_tests stores integration scenarios;
experiments stores version manifests and recordings. Packages may be combined if these
responsibility boundaries and the absence of cyclic dependencies are preserved.
Choose one flow: mock_joint/imu → estimate → control_request → hardware. The monitor receives
independent freshness indicators and permits or prohibits activation. The command interface has
one owner; the diagnostic dashboard does not bypass it to write commands. For every graph edge,
record message type, units, QoS, frequency, time source and stale-data behaviour. Namespaces
allow a second hand instance to launch without changing source code.

Aligning time
Within a process, measure durations with a monotonic clock. A ROS message timestamp provides a
shared time reference and supports TF; across multiple machines, declare the synchronisation
method or restrict one-way latency measurement to one machine. Do not subtract clocks from
different devices and call the difference transport latency without estimating their offset.
Separately measure publication period, callback start, callback duration and data age when a
command is calculated.
Changes to system time or /clock must not silently change freshness monitoring. Choose virtual
time in advance for replay, and a defined stable time base for hardware freshness checks. When
replay pauses, controller behaviour must match the selected mode. Indefinitely repeating an old
physical command is unacceptable. Test replay on the simulator using a separate namespace and
configuration.

Reproducibility and documentation
One command builds the workspace, another starts the simulator and a third runs checks. State
dependencies, tested OS and architecture, terminal procedure and expected topics. The report
contains the graph, QoS table, lifecycle diagram, callback measurements, recorded failure and a
test link. Building from a clean directory checks paths to installed resources, rather than accidental
absolute paths from your computer.
Dashboard snapshots provide a quick overview, but the bag, manifest and calculated metrics
remain the primary evidence. The project rests on clear responsibilities: who produces data, who
makes decisions and who may operate the actuator.

Six integration and fault tests

Delivery and execution
F1 - incompatible QoS. Launch a publisher and subscriber with conflicting reliability or durability
requirements and demonstrate the missing expected exchange. Expected: a diagnostic
explanation rather than “the sensor is broken”. Then fix the profile and repeat the same
experiment with N packets, saving counters.
F2 - slow callback. Artificial processing takes longer than the source period. Expected: queue size
and data age grow, or messages are lost according to the profile; measure p99 and the maximum.
Explain how executor and callback-group choices affect the result. An average frequency close to
the target does not confirm data freshness.
F3 - source restart. The source resets seq and temporarily disappears. Expected: a new session_id
distinguishes restart from counter overflow or packet reordering; freshness monitoring detects the
pause; data returning does not automatically activate the device. Record recovery state as a
separate event.

Geometry, hardware layer and replay
F4 - TF from the future or past, and an incorrect coordinate frame. Supply one measurement packet
outside the available buffer and another with an unknown frame_id. Expected: the failure reason is
visible, and no command is generated from an unverified transform. In the corrected case, a known
foot point agrees with an independent calculation within the declared tolerance.
F5 - configure, read or write error. Supply an invalid joint limit, then simulate a hardware error in
the active simulator. Expected: premature activation is prohibited, the cause is diagnosed, and
controllers do not silently keep writing to an invalid interface. Fix the specific sequence of controller
and hardware-component actions according to the installed ros2_control version and the test.
F6 - an incomplete bag and a shutdown hang. Remove a required stream or stop one node before
the scenario ends. Expected: the replay test detects the missing event or data and ends at its
timeout; all processes terminate. A repeated run inherits neither old nodes nor an accidental /clock
from the previous test.

Confirming a correction
Each negative test has a corrected counterpart with identical initial conditions. Record the injected
error, observed event, detection delay, final state and path to the bag or trace. If the cause
remains unknown, leave the status open. Do not substitute increases to every timeout for a
correction: any new time limit must be justified by measurements and control requirements.
Virtual-machine special case: participant discovery may depend on network mode, while tracing
depends on the kernel and access to system mechanisms. Do not hide these environment
limitations under “everything checked”; still complete console and simulator checks, and prepare a
separate rerun plan for any unavailable hardware check.

Acceptance and preparation for a digital twin

Demonstrating the working stack
From a clean environment, build the packages, launch two namespaces and show IMU and joint
data flow and action cancellation. Enable a slow callback and explain what happens using metrics.
Finish by replaying a fault bag and checking that the automated test detected the expected event.
Show RViz, counters, hardware-layer state and the absence of processes left running.
Required acceptance checks: build and launch are reproducible; units and data ownership are
documented; QoS is compatible and tested; TF uses measurement time; the simulator handles a
fault correctly and enters the specified state. The recording contains required streams,
repeatability criteria are explicit, and automated tests terminate on both success and failure.
Starting a node alone does not confirm correct command execution.

Self-check
1) When should you use an action instead of a service? 2) Why do parameters not replace a
command stream? 3) How does incompatible QoS appear in the graph? 4) Why does reliable not
mean fresh? 5) How does deadline differ from application-level freshness monitoring? 6) Why do
multiple threads not rescue one mutually exclusive callback group?
7) How do you obtain a transform at measurement time? 8) Why should visual and collision differ?
9) Who owns the command interface? 10) What happens after a read or write error in your
particular stack? 11) Which data outside the bag are needed for repetition? 12) Why can the same
bag run with different delays? Support answers with configuration, a log or a test.

Handover to M13 and the G2 boundary
Pass on the leg Xacro, joint names, hardware interfaces, launch, QoS, bag template and fault
catalogue. This is the framework for a digital twin in Gazebo: the model will replace the hardware
simulator while preserving external interfaces and their requirements. Initial quadruped geometry
in M13 will extend one validated leg; a physical quadruped is not yet required.
Architectural and replay material is ready for G2, but the overall plan also includes a one-leg HIL
criterion developed in M13. This criterion remains open until an experiment with real hardware; a
software simulator does not satisfy it. Reaching the G2 calendar date does not justify assigning
PASS to every item.
If behind schedule, reduce the dashboard to four plots and retain one hardware simulator. Preserve
QoS and execution-time measurements, TF at measurement time and a reproducible failure. If
physical-interface validation or tracing is deferred, state the environment, command, expected file
and specific untested criterion.

Assigned reading: a focused route through
the sections

The references are mainly in English; keep the plan and definitions in English. Read selected
sections before the task rather than whole books. Core reading is included in the existing 35 hours
of analysis; consult references as needed.

Core reading
E. Renard, ROS 2 from Scratch (2024). Book and contents. Chapter 3 Uncovering ROS 2 Core
Concepts; in Chapters 5-9, only sections on custom interfaces, action mechanisms, YAML and
launch configuration. For tasks 1-2, list requirements for a stream, a request and a cancellable
action. Use Chapters 10-12 selectively for TF and URDF.
K. Lynch, F. Park, Modern Robotics (2017). Book and teaching sections. §3.2.1 Rotation
Matrices and §3.3.1 Homogeneous Transformation Matrices. For task 5, construct camera → body →
world and its inverse by hand; this checks the meaning of TF rather than repeating the kinematics
course.

Reference reading
M. van Steen, A. Tanenbaum, Distributed Systems, 3rd ed. (2017). Authors’ contents. In
Chapters 3 Processes, 4 Communication and 8 Fault tolerance, select explanations of threads,
communication and partial failures. For tasks 3-4 and 8, distinguish delivery, execution, timeliness
and recovery; the entire textbook is unnecessary.

Three practical references
ROS 2 Design: QoS - DDS Quality of Service policies and QoS profiles; task 3, compatibility table.
Check values and actual behaviour against Jazzy.
Jazzy: Using time in tf2 - Background and Tasks; task 5, requesting a transform at measurement
time and handling its absence.
ros2_control: Hardware Components - lifecycle and state and command interfaces; task 6, one

Terms and definitions

Node. A logical ROS component with its own interfaces; several nodes may execute inside one
process.
ROS graph. The collection of nodes and their communication links discovered within a specified
domain and accessible network.
Topic. A named, typed message stream; publication generally does not acknowledge command
execution by a subscriber.
Service. A request-response interaction for a bounded operation; an action is more suitable for a
long motion with cancellation.
Action. A protocol for a long-running task with feedback, a result and a cancellation request that
the server must handle.
Parameter. A named node setting with a type and change rules; it does not replace a sequence of
control commands.
Namespace. A ROS-name prefix that separates subsystem instances; it does not itself provide
network isolation.
DDS and RMW. DDS implements data exchange; the RMW layer connects ROS to a specific
middleware implementation.
QoS. A set of message-delivery and storage policies; offered and requested profiles must be
compatible for communication.
Reliability. A best effort or reliable delivery policy; retransmission does not guarantee message
freshness by the control deadline.
Durability. A policy governing availability of previously published data to late subscribers;
transient local requires care for motion commands.
Deadline. The expected maximum interval between updates; a violation event reports a problem
and the application implements the response.
Executor. A mechanism that selects ready callbacks and executes them on available threads
according to group configuration.
Callback group. A group of callback functions governed by mutual exclusion or permission for
concurrent execution.
Deadlock. A state of mutual waiting for resources or events in which participants cannot continue
execution.
Timestamp. The numerical time of an event on a specified time scale; measurement time may
differ from message-receipt time.
TF2. A buffered tree of coordinate transforms that supports queries for relationships between
frames at a specified time.
Lifecycle. Explicit component configuration, activation, deactivation and error states, with
permitted transitions between them.
Hardware interface. A contract for reading a resource’s state and writing its command; control
authority must belong to a defined owner.
Replay. Resupplying recorded data with known timing and configuration; the original computation
order may not be reproduced.

Motion fundamentals: the physical meaning
of URDF

Required: four descriptions of one link
Which torques will the leg shown in RViz require? Kinematics specifies connections, axes and
transforms; visual specifies appearance; collision specifies collision and contact geometry; inertial
specifies mass, centre of mass and inertia. Before starting, transform one point between two
coordinate frames and explain the distinction between kg and kg·m².
robot_state_publisher computes TF from geometry and joint positions without integrating the
effects of forces. ros2_control defines command and state interfaces. The name effort does not
turn a position servo into a controllable torque source.

One bar: geometry and inertia from dimensions
Base B is fixed in a right-handed frame: x forward, z upward. The joint at the origin rotates about
+y. At q = 0, the link points along +x; positive q lowers its endpoint: p_tip in B = (L cos q, 0, −L
sin q). For L = 0.20 m, q = 0 gives (0.20; 0; 0) m and q = π/2 gives (0; 0; −0.20) m. Mass does not
enter this calculation.
A uniform bar has length L = 0.20 m along the link’s x-axis, width and height d = 0.02 m, and m =
0.30 kg. The link-frame origin is at the joint; its centre of mass in this frame is (0.10; 0; 0) m. Place
the inertia frame at the centre of mass with parallel axes:
Ixx = m(d² + d²)/12 = 0.000020 kg·m²;
Iyy = Izz = m(L² + d²)/12 = 0.001010 kg·m².
Off-diagonal elements vanish by symmetry. In inertial, specify this tensor about the COM and the
centre-of-mass origin; also offset the bar’s visual and collision by L/2.
Inertia about the joint axis is I_joint = Iyy + m(L/2)² = 0.004010 kg·m². The added term
accounts for circular COM motion. Substituting I_joint for Iyy while origin is at the COM counts the
axis transfer twice and distorts dynamics.
At q = 0, gravity (0; 0; −mg) N acts at x = 0.10 m. Its moment about +y is +mgL/2 = +0.2943
N·m; holding requires actuator torque −0.2943 N·m. At q = π/2, the force lever arm is zero. The
sign follows from r × f.
Doubling mass without changing shape doubles inertia and gravitational torque; TF at a given q is
unchanged. Changing visual changes appearance. Enlarging collision geometry changes contact
and clearances but does not establish link strength. The bar checks units; real masses and material
distributions come from measurements or CAD.

Two model checks within hours 41-60
1. Select a test link in the leg description; save its URDF and p_tip from manual FK and TF at q = 0
and π/2. The example tolerance is 10⁻⁶ m for this model. 2. Double the mass in a copy; use a script
to check doubled I and torque and unchanged FK. Save mass, COM, the six I elements and
coordinate frames; principal moments of inertia must be positive.
Time: 1 theory hour from 45-46 (URDF, masses, collision geometry), and 1 practice hour each from
51-52 (leg description) and 53-54 (task 5, TF). These check the existing model within the 75 hours.
Contact comes in M13; no real actuator commands are sent here.
Read: Lynch, Park, Modern Robotics §8.2, Part 1 - COM and inertia; ROS urdf_tutorial,
07-physics.urdf - visual, collision and inertial structure. Do not copy placeholder inertias from XML:
they are calculated here.
