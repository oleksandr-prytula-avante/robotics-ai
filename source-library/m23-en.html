Six legs: scale only what has been verified

M23 • 01 Oct 2028 - 01 Nov 2028 • 75 hours

The completed robot
M23 aims to produce a six-legged robot v1.0 with 18 degrees of freedom, slow walking
and active body levelling: three actuators per leg. It must stand independently for at least 120
seconds, perform wave and ripple gaits under declared conditions, and diagnose loss of data,
contacts and individual components. The central question is what changes when moving from four
legs to six in mechanics, power, communication timing and support control.
18 DOF refers to the number of actuated joint coordinates, not the number of independent body
motions available on the ground. Additional legs provide more contact-allocation options, but
increase mass, energy consumption, wiring and possible faults. Simply appending six IDs to an
array does not validate previous calculations. Analysing the architectural change is a major
practical task.

Entry check before scaling
Open the M22 package: independent quadruped standing, slow gait, functioning independent stop,
calibrations, current and bus budgets, and analysis of significant failures. Record the entry decision
PASS/HOLD in writing. If E-stop, power, actuator identification or the control cycle remain
unverified, physical expansion stays on HOLD. In that case, complete M23 calculations and
simulation tasks while resolving hardware blockers.
The six additional actuators and parts for two legs are planned only after this check, as in the
original plan. Purchasing is not automatic: first verify compatibility, calculate the budget and obtain
the project owner's decision. Changing actuator family for the last two legs requires new
calibrations, a delay model and verification of joint operation.

The operating scope of the result
The ±10-15° range in the overall plan is a design target for body levelling and slope tests,
admissible only where the particular assembly's geometry, stability, traction and loads permit. Do
not adopt it as an operating range before calculation. Start with a smaller verified angle and
expand only when criteria are met. State surface angle and desired body angle separately: they
are different quantities.
The robotic hand develops along its own OPT workstream after M17; M23 leg verification does not
replace its limits or results. Shared logging and diagnostics benefit both systems. The final goal is a
walking robot and hand by programme completion, so pass six-legged-version limitations and
unfinished hand items to M24 and the separate G4 without adding hidden hours.

Work blocks, measurements and scope
management

The monthly plan: 35 hours of analysis + 40 hours of practice
1-20: 10 hours recalculating mechanics, power and communication; 10 hours assembling,
calibrating and testing 18 devices. 21-40: 10 hours on contact sequences and admissible
transitions; 10 hours implementing wave and ripple in simulation and on the prepared test area.
41-60: 10 hours on surface, contact and uncertainty estimation; 10 hours on active levelling,
bounded terrain and operation without a camera. 61-75: 5 hours analysing reliability and energy;
10 hours conducting a test series, maintenance and preparing v1.0.
Organise the first three analysis blocks as five 2-hour sessions each; the last is 2 + 2 + 1. Practice
comprises four Sunday blocks of 10 study hours. Alternate movement, cooling, inspection,
resetting initial conditions and analysis. Charging time alone is not study time; use that interval for
calculations and data processing.

One experiment, one question
For every run, record gait type, phase order, cycle duration, swing height, target body tilt, surface,
load, battery condition and temperatures. Begin by comparing two configurations differing in one
parameter. Simultaneously changing gait, surface and controller gain prevents you from identifying
why error changed.
The first-page dates repeat the overall plan's calendar window. Shared boundary dates do not
double hours; holidays and integration G blocks retain their place in the overall schedule.
Work-hour numbers specify task order and do not require extra evening work. Print parts and
arrange components in advance after entry-check authorisation, allowing for actual delivery time.

Reducing scope while preserving the objective
If behind schedule, postpone tripod first, then sideways motion on difficult terrain, then optional
depth-camera comparisons and additional surfaces. Retain wave and ripple, standing, independent
stopping, admissible-slope assessment, operation when the camera is unavailable and transparent
repeated-test results. Without hardware verification of wave and ripple, call the result a candidate,
not a completed six-legged robot v1.0.
Do not consume the hand reserve or G4's 35 hours on minor gait refinements without replanning
the programme. The main deliverable is a justified operating envelope. Python is useful for support
geometry and reports, C++ for the existing control loop. An additional log analyser may be written
in Rust; a third implementation is optional.

The six-legged frame and the 18-actuator
budget

What to study and recalculate
Examine the arrangement of six hip assemblies, fastener access, harness routing and
centre-of-mass changes. Check overlap between adjacent leg workspaces, load transmission from
the middle pair to the frame and whether one actuator can be removed without dismantling the
body. New legs use the existing naming conventions and units but have their own calibrations.
Estimate stiffness for the selected design load case and compare it with admissible foot-position
change.
Build the power budget for actual load combinations: standing, initiating swing, a low body posture
and stopping. Do not assign each leg exactly one sixth of the weight in every phase. List
specification limits and cooling conditions for branches, connectors, BMS and DC-DC converters.
Summed rated peaks represent only one conservative scenario; separately measure simultaneous
currents and their duration.

Task 1. Design review: 4 → 6
Inputs are the M22 candidate, six-legged-frame CAD and component specifications. 1) List changes
to parts, masses, axes, harnesses and power distribution. 2) Check fasteners, clearances and
service access for every new hip frame. 3) Calculate the new configuration's centre of mass and
predict support reactions. 4) After unpowered assembly, measure mass and verify geometry using
reference dimensions.
Save scaleup_design_review.md and an updated assembly_manifest. Check that every change
traces to a CAD revision and that software renaming does not conceal workspace collisions.
Boundary case: a middle foot collides with a front link during turning. Correct the trajectory or
geometry and save the forbidden region as a test before permitting physical motion.

Task 2. Power and communication after expansion
Calculate the 18-actuator communication cycle for your protocol and telemetry content, then
compare with M22 measurements for 12 devices. With the robot supported, add new branches in
stages, checking IDs, polarity, signs, zeros and idle current. Measure simultaneous communication
at the actual frequency, computer load and enabled diagnostics. Record supply and branch
currents separately where instruments permit.
Save power_bus_18.csv and plots of margin to the communication deadline. Acceptance: new
devices create no duplicate IDs, and voltage sag and delays remain within justified operating
ranges. Boundary case: two branches work separately but overload a shared DC-DC converter
together. Revisit calculation and measurement of the common circuit; reducing gait speed is not a
universal solution to an electrical problem.

Wave, ripple and admissible contact
transitions

Gait model and transition conditions
Describe each leg with the state machine STANCE → LIFT → SWING → TOUCHDOWN → STANCE. In
STANCE, the foot is an assumed support; in SWING it moves; TOUCHDOWN requires contact
confirmation. Transitions depend on more than time: check target reachability, state freshness and
remaining supports. Phase φ from 0 to 1 identifies position in the cycle, while duty factor is the
fraction of the cycle assigned to stance.
For slow motion, calculate the convex polygon of confirmed supports and the centre-of-mass
projection along gravity onto their plane. Support margin is the signed minimum distance from this
projection to the polygon boundaries. On a horizontal floor, this is the ordinary vertical projection;
retain the projection direction on a slope. Positive geometric margin alone does not check friction,
actuator torque or dynamics.

Task 3. A wave table and verifiable support margin
Inputs are the six-legged model, level floor, calibrated axes and initial standing pose. 1) Specify
sequential single-leg swing and a phase table for all six legs. 2) Before lift-off, recalculate
remaining supports and body-position feasibility. 3) Visualise contacts, polygon and margin in
metres. 4) Run a complete cycle, startup and stopping, first in simulation and then with a small
permitted step on the test area.
Save gait_wave.yaml, the phase diagram and a log of minimum margin. Check that no more than
one leg swings at a time, feasible support exists before lift-off and commands cannot skip
touchdown. Boundary case: the foot has not touched down but the timer requests the next lift.
Block the transition and emit an event; the state machine must not continue without contact
confirmation.

Task 4. Ripple, turning and sideways motion
Construct ripple with more overlap between swings, an explicit phase table and allowed
combinations. Do not rely on the gait name alone: ripple implementations use different
conventions. For every combination, check reachability, collisions and support. Then add turning
about body z and sideways translation separately; positive directions follow the frozen coordinate
frame.
Save gait_ripple.yaml, allowed-transition tests and three short scenarios: forwards, turning and
sideways. Check that an identical command has the same meaning for right and left legs and that
mode changes pass through an admissible state. Boundary case: switching wave → ripple during
swing. Permit it only after phase alignment or through a stop. If the assumed contact is physically
impossible, the test fails even when motion appears smooth.

Six contacts, the surface plane and
uncertainty

What to study
Review transforming foot coordinates from leg frames to a common body or world frame. For an
approximately planar patch, estimate z = a·x + b·y + c from confirmed supports: matrix A contains
rows [x, y, 1], vector z contains foot heights in metres, and the solution minimises squared
residuals. The normal is proportional to (−a, −b, 1). Use weights for measurements with different
reliability, explaining their origin.
A plane model cannot represent arbitrary stairs or separate rocks without examining residuals.
Three nearly collinear points provide poor geometry even with a small computed error. Six
coordinates are not six independent measurements if all derive from one erroneous body
orientation. Contact, a stationary foot and estimator confidence are three different indicators.

Task 5. Estimate a plane from six supports
Generate synthetic data for a horizontal plane and two inclined planes with known parameters,
then add noise, one outlier and contact loss. 1) Transform every foot into one frame. 2) Select
confirmed supports. 3) Solve by least squares and check rank and conditioning. 4) Calculate
residuals and a validity flag. 5) Compare with an independent reference measurement of the
prepared area.
Save terrain_estimator_tests and plot_plane_error. Check that ideal data recover the specified
plane within numerical precision; with noise, compare error to the declared tolerance, not zero.
Boundary cases are two supports, nearly collinear contacts and one foot on a protrusion. Return
invalidity or a limited estimate in these cases; an arbitrary normal without a flag is unacceptable.

Task 6. Depth-based foothold selection and camera failure
Use a saved scene depth map and camera calibration from earlier modules. Construct a small
region of admissible footholds: reachable kinematics, clearance, known surface, height uncertainty
and image age. Compare selection against a method using only confirmed contacts. With the
camera disabled, the robot remains in a previously tested mode or stops; an IMU alone does not
authorise movement over unknown terrain.
Save foothold_candidates.json and two identical scenarios with and without the camera. Check that
unknown depth does not become zero height, a stale image is not treated as fresh, and the
selected foothold passes reachability checks. Boundary case: a shiny or dark surface creates a hole
in the depth map. Show uncertainty and explain rejection of the placement while retaining the
classical fallback mode.

Active levelling and the terrain operating
boundary

Relating body tilt to leg configuration
Levelling maintains a selected body orientation relative to gravity by changing leg positions within
reachability and load limits. The IMU estimates orientation, contact estimation describes support,
and kinematics converts desired body position into joint commands. Zero roll and pitch do not
mean the feet lie at the same height; on a slope, leg configurations may differ.
Map the constraints for each pose: joint travel, proximity to singularity, loaded torque, body
clearance, traction, contact quality and support margin. Estimate quasi-static joint torque using
T·f, where J is the foot Jacobian, f is force in newtons and the result is in N·m. Force allocation
J(q)
must satisfy equilibrium and contact constraints; equal forces on all six legs are only one special
case.

Task 7. Verify 120 seconds of standing
Start on a level surface in a previously verified configuration. 1) Run a short current and contact
check. 2) Stand independently for at least 120 seconds without the backup support carrying
weight. 3) Record roll and pitch errors, data freshness, minimum support margin and temperatures.
4) Repeat after cooling and restoring initial conditions, explicitly identifying every repeat.
Save stand_120s with raw log and video. The criterion is a complete continuous interval within
declared limits, without body support or unaccounted measurement losses. This verifies a
particular standing condition, not machine lifetime. Boundary case: tilt error decreases by
saturating one joint. Body-orientation error is small, but a constraint is violated, so the trial fails.

Task 8. Map slopes and uneven terrain
Before the physical trial, calculate admissible surface angles and commanded body orientations,
then test them in simulation with mass, friction and delay variations. On an adjustable rig with
suitable backup support, increase slope in steps from a verified value. For each angle, save
achieved body roll and pitch, currents, joint poses, contacts and stopping reason. Introduce
unevenness as a separate factor with measured height.
Save operating_envelope.csv: tested, untested and forbidden regions. Mark the ±10-15° design
target as achieved only from actual tests of the specific configuration. If the limit is smaller, identify
the limiting mechanism. Boundary case: traction is lost while torque remains admissible. Reducing
controller error does not solve a contact problem. Return to level ground and investigate
foot-surface interaction.
When expanding the M20 force allocator, update contact-set size, constraints and solver timing
measurements. In HIL, test infeasible constraints and solver-deadline overruns: retain the
previously tested classical fallback. The M21 policy is outside the mandatory physical result;
transfer from four legs to six requires separate verification of observations, actions and
applicability.

Reliability, energy and maintenance

A fault tree with a clear top event
Choose the top event: the robot loses admissible support or fails to stop on request. Divide causes
into power, bus, actuator, mechanical connection, state estimation and control logic. AND denotes
a combination of required events; OR denotes alternative sufficient paths. Do not assign branch
probabilities without data; a qualitative tree already helps select tests. A common power source or
configuration error links several legs, so do not silently assume independent failures.
With only five functioning legs, the system must first recognise the condition and restrict
behaviour. Continued walking depends on geometry, load and how the sixth leg failed. A freely
dangling leg, a leg jammed in the air and a leg jammed while supporting load require different
responses. The basic fallback to verify is stopping or lowering according to the design procedure.
Five-legged walking requires a separate justification.

Task 9. Component or leg failures and recovery
From the tree, select telemetry loss, a stopped node and incorrect contact. First inject them in
simulation or HIL, recording the stimulus time, detection and resulting state. Then reproduce only
permissible rig variants with the robot supported. Check that a recovered node cannot receive an
old command without state verification and an explicit resumption procedure.
Save fault_tree and fault_runs with a timeline. Check that every stimulus has an expected outcome
and measured detection time; behaviour must fit the remaining contacts and limits. Boundary
case: a common bus failure produces six identical warnings instead of recognising a shared cause.
Add aggregate status while retaining primary errors. Do not unplug a loaded connector by hand to
demonstrate a fault.

Task 10. Energy and maintainability
On a marked route, measure voltage U(t), supply current I(t) and distance d. Energy E = ∫U·I dt is in
joules; E/3600 is Wh, and E/d is J/m for a valid non-zero distance. Compare wave and ripple at
comparable speed, surface, mass and temperature. For a stationary robot, report power and
energy over a selected interval; do not divide by a near-zero distance.
Save energy_comparison.csv with integration boundaries, measurement gaps and repeats. Then,
with the system de-energised, replace an accessible service assembly according to its instructions,
measure the time, and repeat inspection and calibration. Save maintenance_runbook and a
reasonable spare-parts list based on actual failures. Boundary case: the mechanics are restored
but zero is lost. Restart is prohibited until the joint record is updated.
For thermal balance, tabulate every actuator's temperatures on the same route: initial, maximum
observed, rise and load duration. Identify a consistently overloaded leg and check zero, friction and
support allocation. Change only one admissible configuration or phase parameter and repeat;
reduced heating in one leg must not conceal increased heating elsewhere or compromised support.

The test series and six required boundary
cases

A repeat plan with a defined trial unit
Plan 40 attempts as a working target within the original 30-50+ guideline. Example allocation: 10
wave trials on level ground, 10 ripple trials on the same ground, 10 levelling trials on a verified
slope, and 10 transitions into restricted operation without a camera. Each attempt includes
restoring the starting pose, checking conditions, executing the specified scenario and recording the
outcome. Sensor samples and steps within one traversal are not independent attempts.
If equipment availability or cooling duration prevents this plan, justify a smaller sample and
narrower conclusion in advance. Do not replace unavailable difficult trials with many identical easy
ones while claiming the original coverage. Mark untested conditions explicitly. For comparisons, fix
the order or alternate modes so battery discharge is not mistaken for a gait advantage.

Control scenarios
T1. Camera loss during an admissible short route: enter a verified fallback or stop, without
selecting new unknown footholds. T2. One false contact during replay: the estimator accounts for
inconsistency and does not automatically expand support. T3. Nearly collinear surface points:
return poor-conditioning or invalidity status; do not present an arbitrary slope as a reliable
estimate.
T4. Stale state from one leg: do not authorise the next phase using an old pose. T5. Wave/ripple
switching in an inconvenient phase: defer the transition or pass through a stop, retaining an
admissible contact order. T6. A stopped node or bus loss in HIL: detection, restricted behaviour and
recovery proceed without unintended resumption. A physical variant requires impact analysis and
support verification first.

What the results can establish
Report N, successes, failures and unusable records separately by condition. Calculate a confidence
interval for success rate and explicitly state the assumption of independent trials. Even 40
successful tests do not prove absolute reliability or determine lifetime in hours. Temperature drift
and a shared defect in one assembly make trials dependent; discuss this limitation instead of
artificially increasing N.
The summary must include at least one negative scenario with the expected restriction of
behaviour and one real failure if one occurred. Analyse causes, add a regression test and repeat
the relevant test class. A version change during the series separates data into different
configurations; do not combine them into one v1.0 estimate without explanation.

Hours 1-10: decisions and calculations

blocks power-up, limits the tested mode or concerns future expansion. Separately assess the

have a concrete task 1 work list and expected task 2 measurements.

Hours 11-20: expansion on the rig
11-14: assemble new hip frames and two legs; measure mass, add labels and check clearances.

load and power measurements with 18 devices. 20: repeat independent stopping, inspect and
freeze the revision. If the entry decision is HOLD, use these hours to repair the quadruped and test
the six-legged model without physical expansion.
Post-assembly verification must include the original four legs: new harnesses or added mass can
impair previously working components. Repeating known M22 scenarios checks preservation of
earlier functions, but does not verify an untested six-legged gait. Update numerical limits only with
supporting evidence.

Hours 21-40: contacts and movement
21-22: phase state machine and duty factor. 23-24: support polygon, centre-of-mass projection and
permissible-lift criteria. 25-26: wave table and touchdown that does not occur when the timer
expects it. 27-28: ripple and allowed overlapping swings. 29-30: turning, sideways motion and gait

31-34: task 3 in simulation, support checks and a short hardware wave traversal after rig testing.
35-37: task 4, ripple and transition through an aligned phase. 38-39: separate turning and sideways
scenarios within the verified region. 40: analyse worst support margin and correct one transition
fault. Treat tripod as a possible extension; do not add it at the expense of unfinished touchdown
control.

Hours 41-50: four important distinctions
41-42: foot coordinate frames, gravity and the support plane. 43-44: least squares, residuals, rank
and nearly collinear contacts. 45-46: contact confidence, slip and body observability. 47-48: depth
uncertainty, image age and selection of a reachable foothold. 49-50: load and kinematic-constraint
maps on a slope, desired body orientation and conditions for restricting motion.
Explain four distinctions in writing: touching versus stationary support; the plane beneath the feet
versus the horizontal; body tilt versus surface slope; and no camera versus no sensors at all. If
these concepts are confused, postpone new-terrain trials and correct notation and tests.
Terminology must agree across code, plots and protocol.

Hours 51-60: the surface experiment
51-53: task 5 on synthetic data and the measured area, including an outlier and contact loss.
54-55: task 6, the saved depth map and the same scenario without a camera. 56-57: preliminary

but count it in the confirmation series only if it follows the predefined protocol.
A failed slope trial still yields a useful result: the operating boundary and its cause. Do not raise
current limits to reach a desired plot value without recalculating the complete power circuit and
thermal behaviour. Do not change foot size midway through a series without a new design revision
either: traction and the contact model then change.

Hours 61-75: reliability and release
61-62: fault tree, five functioning legs and admissible recovery. 63-64: energy metrics, a 30-50+
series or justified N, accounting for repeats and dependence. 65: readiness of scenarios,

66-68: task 9 and control faults first in HIL, then necessary rig checks. 69-71: the prepared series of

candidate with release blockers. Do not shorten checks merely to reach a repeat count: revise
claimed precision and coverage.

Acceptance, additional gaits and the M24
handoff

Required evidence for v1.0
The package includes the six-legged-frame revision and 18-joint map; mass and centre of mass;
power and communication calculations and measurements; repeated independent-stop
verification; confirmed 120-second standing; wave and ripple with admissible phases; levelling
within a defined region; responses to camera loss and node failure; a test series with justified N; an
energy report; and inspection and maintenance instructions.
Every result has a raw file, configuration version and criterion. Holding level becomes a measured
roll/pitch error, surface condition and duration. Traversing uneven terrain requires measured
obstacle geometry and attempt count. Operation without a camera applies only to the tested
restricted mode. An unmeasured quantity remains unknown even when the video appears
convincing.

When to investigate tripod
Consider tripod only after verifying wave and ripple, actuator loading and feasibility of three-legged
support. Typical alternating groups for a symmetric six-legged body are LF/RM/LR and RF/LM/RR;
verify this convention against your names and geometry. For every phase, check contacts, margin
and transition conditions. A triangle's existence does not guarantee admissible torques, friction or
stability during swing.
Optional task if time permits: compare tripod and ripple phase tables in simulation with the same
input, saving minimum margin and errors. A physical run requires a separate positive decision for
that mode; it is outside the required M23 evidence. Similarly, five-legged walking remains research
unless its operating envelope is calculated and verified.

Defence and handoff package
Answer: why do 18 actuators change the communication deadline; why is six-legged load not
always shared equally; what does insufficient rank in plane estimation mean; how do loss of
contact and slip differ; why does zero tilt not guarantee an admissible pose; what response is
available for a jammed leg; what does E/d measure; and why do 40 successful attempts not mean
100% reliability? Demonstrate the answers using your data.
Pass to M24 requirements_candidates.csv, the attempt register, links to every raw log, assembly
and calibration instructions, limitations, known defects and repair priorities. Record the hand's
status and OPT results on a separate line. Do not rename an unfinished function as verified to meet
the shared date. G4 remains a separate 35-hour integration checkpoint after the modules; it is not
hidden within these 75 hours.

Assigned reading: six legs, contact and
reliability

The references may be in English; the exercises and definitions are presented here in English. Read
only the named sections before the associated work, within the existing 75 hours.

Core reading: contact and loading
1. R. Tedrake - Underactuated Robotics, MIT notes. Planning and Control through Contact →
(Autonomous) Hybrid Systems. For tasks 3-4, write out foot modes, transition conditions (guards)
and state changes on switching (reset); a phase transition requires a verified event. Retain the
remaining contact-optimisation material as reference.
2. K. Lynch, F. Park - Modern Robotics. 12.1.2 Contact Types; 12.1.3 Multiple Contacts; 12.2.1

Terms and definitions

Gait phase. Normalised position within a repeating cycle; it specifies the contact plan but does not
confirm actual foot-surface contact.
Stance. An interval of planned leg contact during which admissible support force is constrained by
traction, geometry and actuators.
Swing. Movement of a foot to its next support without planned support-force transmission;
premature contact requires a separate response.
Wave gait. A slow sequential gait with legs swinging in turn; the specific order and admissible
overlaps are defined by a phase table.
Ripple gait. A gait with phase-shifted waves of swing; the selected table, rather than the name,
determines the number of simultaneous swings.
Tripod gait. A gait alternating two groups of three legs; group geometry alone does not guarantee
admissible forces.
Guard. A testable condition for changing a discrete mode, such as confirmed contact or a timeout;
activation is associated with a defined response.
Reset. A specified model or controller state change at a mode transition, such as clearing a timer
and initialising a new support point.
Support polygon. The convex hull of selected contact points in a planar model; its applicability
depends on assumptions about surface and forces.
Static stability margin. The minimum signed distance from the centre-of-mass projection to the
support-polygon boundary, positive inside. Its use is limited to a planar quasi-static model.
Duty factor β. The fraction of a cycle a leg spends in planned stance; a dimensionless quantity
between zero and one.
Surface normal. A unit vector perpendicular to a local plane; fix its direction and coordinate
frame to prevent sign reversal of the estimate.
System rank. The number of independent data or constraint directions; insufficient rank means
the selected plane parameters cannot be determined uniquely.
Slip. Relative tangential motion between a contacting foot and surface; a contact sensor may
remain active during slipping.
Active levelling. Changing leg commands to reduce body-orientation error while respecting
reachability, contacts and load limits.
Load redistribution. Changing forces among stance legs during movement or failure; additional
contacts do not automatically equalise forces.
Bus utilisation. The fraction of available channel time occupied by communication, including
overhead, pauses and retries; distinct from useful data throughput.
Specific energy E/d. Energy used per unit distance travelled, usually J/m; comparison requires
identical conditions and a non-zero measured distance.

Relationships: β = T_stance/T_cycle; E = ∫U(t)I(t)dt. Check: verified 120-second standing characterises the
completed trial, not every possible duration.

Motion fundamentals: support transitions
and equilibrium

Required analysis: support changes, body acceleration and actual contact

Walking changes both leg angles and the set of active contacts. A lifted foot no longer supplies
support force; count a new support only after confirmed touchdown. The gait timer specifies intent;
the sensor confirms the physical transition. Even six functioning legs do not guarantee stability
when these transitions occur in the wrong order.

From static projection to a moving body
For slow standing on one horizontal plane, check that the centre-of-mass projection lies inside the
contact polygon, friction is sufficient and loads are admissible. Under acceleration, this is
insufficient. In a simplified planar model with constant height h and zero rate of change of angular
momentum about the centre of mass: x_CoP=x_c-(h/g)·a_x. CoP is the point of application of the
resultant normal reaction; it must lie in the admissible support region.
Numerical counterexample. x_c=0.03 m, h=0.25 m, g=9.81 m/s²; after a support transition, the
admissible longitudinal CoP interval is [-0.08; 0.12] m. At rest, CoP=0.03 m. Requesting a_x=5 m/s²
gives CoP=-0.09742 m: the centre-of-mass projection is inside, but the required reaction lies
outside. This model rejects the command. This is a calculation test, not an instruction to accelerate
the physical robot.
For μ=0.6, the aggregate friction constraint gives |a_x|≤μg=5.886 m/s² when vertical acceleration
is zero. It separately permits 5 m/s², but the CoP condition is already violated. Both conditions and
actuator constraints must hold. A longitudinal interval does not check lateral stability; use complete
contact geometry for the robot.

Practice: verify a wave → ripple transition
In the existing simulation scenario, select the instant before leg lift-off. Construct current and next
support regions using confirmed contacts only and calculate static margin. Then estimate required
CoP under the declared assumptions; for the general case, use M20 force constraints. Inject loss of
one contact: defer swing or select a previously verified fallback.
Save contact_transition.csv and support diagrams: phase commands, touchdown confirmations,
x_c, CoP estimate, boundaries and decision reason. Separately test delayed swing and a false early
touchdown. Increasing duty factor provides more time in stance, but does not establish that every
transition is feasible or friction sufficient.

Within 75 hours: 1 hour from 23-24 and 2 hours from the simulation portion of 31-34, tasks 3-4. Physical trials
remain within the accepted M22 region. Read Russ Tedrake, Highly-articulated Legged Robots: CoP and ZMP -
assumptions of simplified dynamics. Do not apply this formula to contacts at different heights or large body
rotations without checking its validity.
